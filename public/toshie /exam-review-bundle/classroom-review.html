How to Spot the Word Type
COMO IDENTIFICAR O TIPO DE PALAVRA · OLHE O QUE VEM LOGO ANTES E LOGO DEPOIS DA LACUNA
NOUNsubstantivo
Usually comes right after a / an / the · my / your / his / her / its / our / their · this / that / these / those · some / many / few · or a possessive 's.the decision · her kindness · three activities · the bridge's failure
VERBverbo
Usually comes right after a subject (I / you / he / she / it / we / they) · a modal (can / will / must / should) · to (infinitive) · an auxiliary (do / does / did / have / has) · or an adverb of frequency (always / never / usually).she decided · will simplify · to arrive · always lets
ADJECTIVEadjetivo
Usually comes right before a noun, or right after a "be"-type verb (is / was / seems / becomes / looks / feels).a kind gesture · she seems calm · it became clear
ADVERBadvérbio
Often ends in -ly · modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb · can move around the sentence more freely than the others.quickly decided · extremely kind · fairly often
Dica: antes de preencher a lacuna, pergunte "o que vem logo antes, e logo depois?" — isso quase sempre entrega a categoria certa.
01 Nominalisation Speed Round 12 items

Complete each sentence with the correct noun form of the word in brackets. The clue in the sentence tells you which suffix fits.

Reminder · the 7 suffixes turn a verb or adjective into a noun. A possessive ("the bridge's ___") or an article ("a ___") right before the gap is almost always a signal that a noun belongs there.
Quick reference · 7 suffixes
-tion/-siondecide → decision · protect → protection
-mentarrange → arrangement · develop → development
-nesskind → kindness · calm → calmness
-thwide → width · deep → depth
-ityactive → activity · curious → curiosity
-urefail → failure · depart → departure
-alarrive → arrival · renew → renewal
1.
The hotel manager needed to make a quick (decide) — three guests were already waiting in the rain. decision
2.
After the renovation, the tea room's (arrange) was completely different — the low table now faced the garden instead of the wall. arrangement
3.
Her (kind) toward the lost tourists — she walked fifteen minutes out of her way — is still mentioned in reviews. kindness
4.
The old stone arch was twenty centimetres too narrow for the truck's (wide). width
5.
The trail closes for six months a year because of volcanic (active) near the summit. activity
6.
The trip nearly ended in (fail) — the ferry to the island was cancelled twice before they finally reached the port. failure
7.
Her (arrive) at the ryokan was delayed by four hours after the mountain pass flooded. arrival
8.
The museum's new (extend) added three galleries devoted entirely to Edo-period ceramics. extension
9.
The shrine's (develop) plan includes replacing all two hundred stone lanterns over the next decade. development
10.
His (calm) during the earthquake — he simply finished pouring the tea before checking on the guests — became a small local legend. calmness
11.
The waterfall's (deep) — nearly forty metres at its centre — makes it one of the most dangerous in the prefecture. depth
12.
There was open (curious) among the villagers about why a foreigner had chosen to restore the abandoned inn. curiosity
02 Word Families · Build the Verb 12 items

Turn the adjective in brackets into a verb. Some need a suffix (-ize/-ise, -en, -ify) · some stay exactly the same (zero-suffix).

Reminder · these verbs are built from adjectives. Ask: does the base word already work as a verb (warm, calm, cool — zero-suffix), or does it need -ize/-ise, -en, or -ify?
Quick reference · verb formation
-ize / -isemodern → modernize · popular → popularize
-enwide → widen · dark → darken · weak → weaken
-ifysimple → simplify · clear → clarify · intense → intensify
zero-suffixwarm → warm · calm → calm · cool → cool
1.
The tea master will (simple) the ceremony for beginners, cutting the steps from twelve to five. simplify
2.
As the sun set behind the mountain, the sky seemed to (dark) in stages — first orange, then deep violet. darken
3.
The visitor centre aims to (modern) the way tourists experience the shrine, without changing the building itself. modernize / modernise
4.
Please (calm) down before we start the ceremony — rushing spoils the whole ritual. calm
5.
The onsen's mineral water is said to (soft) the skin after just one soak. soften
6.
The guide had to (clear) his instructions twice — the fog made it hard to see the trail markers. clarify
7.
The innkeeper will (warm) the futons before guests arrive, using a small heater under each mattress. warm
8.
The council plans to (wide) the mountain road by two metres to let tour buses through. widen
9.
Nothing could (cool) the tension between the two fishing villages after the border dispute. cool
10.
The temple hopes to (popular) the lesser-known autumn festival, which draws far fewer visitors than the spring one. popularize / popularise
11.
Years of salt wind had begun to (weak) the old wooden pier, and the council finally closed it. weaken
12.
The chef tried to (intense) the broth's flavour by simmering the bones for eighteen hours instead of six. intensify
03 Causatives · Which Verb Fits? 12 items

Fill each gap with the causative verb that matches the situation: make (force) · let (permission) · get (persuade, effort) · have (arrange, it's their role) · help (assist).

Reminder · make = force, no choice · let = permission · get = persuade, takes effort (+ to) · have = arrange, it's their role · help = assist, together. The verb form that follows changes with each one.

concierge · コンシェルジュ · recepcionista de hotel
glazier · ガラス職人 · vidraceiro
apprentice · 見習い · aprendiz
current (noun) · 潮流 · corrente (da água)
raft · いかだ · jangada
1.
The strict ryokan owner makes every guest remove their shoes before stepping onto the tatami — no exceptions, ever.
2.
She finally let her son stay up late for the fireworks festival, even though it was a school night.
3.
The concierge had the porter carry the bags up to the third floor — that's simply his job.
4.
It took three phone calls, but he finally got the airline to move his seat to the window.
5.
The old fisherman helped me carry the nets down to the boat — my hands were full and the path was steep.
6.
Nothing could get the toddler to fall asleep once the ferry started moving.
7.
The strong tide made the small boat drift nearly a kilometre off course before the fishermen noticed.
8.
My grandmother always let me choose the tea bowl myself — she believed the choice mattered more than the bowl.
9.
The hotel manager had the broken window fixed before the next guests arrived — she called the glazier that same morning.
10.
After twenty minutes of gentle persuasion, the guide finally got the nervous hiker to cross the rope bridge.
11.
The volunteers helped the elderly visitors carry their bags across the temple grounds, especially on the steep stone steps.
12.

04 Mixed Integration · Transform 8 items

Rewrite the idea using the structure asked for. This mixes all three areas — read carefully before answering.

Reminder · this section mixes all three grammar points from today. Read each sentence twice before choosing — the clue is usually in the word right before or after the gap.
1.
"The manager decided to close the pool early." → Use a noun. The manager's decision to close the pool early surprised the guests.
2.
"The road became wider after the construction." → Use a verb. The construction widened the road.
3.
"The guide forced the tourists to wear helmets." → Use make. The guide made the tourists wear helmets.
4.
"Her explanation became clearer once she used a map." → Use a verb. The map helped clarify her explanation.
5.
"The chef allowed the apprentice to taste the sauce." → Use let. The chef let the apprentice taste the sauce.
6.
"The volcano's activity became more intense." → Use a verb. The volcano's activity intensified over the past month.
7.
"The hotel arranged for a car to pick up the guests." → Use have. The hotel had a car pick the guests up.
8.
"The failure of the bridge closed the pass for a week." → Use a verb. The bridge failed so badly that the pass closed for a week.
05 Speaking Corner · Oral Review 5 prompts

No writing — just talk. Try to use one structure from today's three grammar points in each answer.

Describe a decision you made recently. Use the word "decision" and one causative verb (make / get / have) in your answer.
Has anyone ever let you do something unusual? Tell the story.
Describe how a place — a room, a garden, a trail — has changed over time. Use a word-family verb (widen, simplify, intensify, weaken, darken…).
Talk about a time someone helped you do something difficult during a trip.
What's something in Japan that has "stood the test of time"? (Think back to the Wabi-Sabi lesson.)
