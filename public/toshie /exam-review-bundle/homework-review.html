How to Spot the Word Type
COMO IDENTIFICAR O TIPO DE PALAVRA · OLHE O QUE VEM LOGO ANTES E LOGO DEPOIS DA LACUNA
NOUNsubstantivo
Usually comes right after a / an / the · my / your / his / her / its / our / their · this / that / these / those · some / many / few · or a possessive 's.the decision · her kindness · three activities · the bridge's failure
VERBverbo
Usually comes right after a subject (I / you / he / she / it / we / they) · a modal (can / will / must / should) · to (infinitive) · an auxiliary (do / does / did / have / has) · or an adverb of frequency (always / never / usually).she decided · will simplify · to arrive · always lets
ADJECTIVEadjetivo
Usually comes right before a noun, or right after a "be"-type verb (is / was / seems / becomes / looks / feels).a kind gesture · she seems calm · it became clear
ADVERBadvérbio
Often ends in -ly · modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb · can move around the sentence more freely than the others.quickly decided · extremely kind · fairly often
Dica: antes de preencher a lacuna, pergunte "o que vem logo antes, e logo depois?" — isso quase sempre entrega a categoria certa.

03 Causatives · Rewrite the Sentence 15 items

Rewrite each sentence using the causative verb given in brackets. Two gaps per item.

Reminder · make = force, no choice · let = permission · get = persuade, takes effort (+ to) · have = arrange, it's their role · help = assist, together. The verb form that follows changes with each one.
Glossary · useful words for this section
porter · ポーター · carregador de bagagem
concierge · コンシェルジュ · recepcionista de hotel
glazier · ガラス職人 · vidraceiro
apprentice · 見習い · aprendiz
current (noun) · 潮流 · corrente (da água)
raft · いかだ · jangada
1.
The manager forced the staff to wear uniforms. (make) → The manager made the staff wear uniforms.
2.
My uncle allowed me to steer the fishing boat for ten minutes. (let) → My uncle let me steer the boat for ten minutes.
3.
The hotel arranged for a doctor to visit the sick guest. (have) → The hotel had a doctor visit the sick guest.
4.
After a long argument, she persuaded the driver to wait ten more minutes. (get) → After a long argument, she got the driver to wait ten more minutes.
5.
The old fisherman assisted the boy in tying the knot correctly. (help) → The old fisherman helped the boy tie / to tie the knot correctly.
6.
The strong current forced the raft to spin twice before it reached the bank. (make) → The strong current made the raft spin twice before it reached the bank.
7.
The strict teacher didn't allow the students to use dictionaries during the test. (let) → The strict teacher didn't let the students use dictionaries during the test.
8.
The receptionist arranged for the porter to bring the luggage up at six. (have) → The receptionist had the porter bring the luggage up at six.
9.
It took real effort, but the guide persuaded the frightened hiker to cross the bridge. (get) → It took real effort, but the guide got the frightened hiker to cross the bridge.
10.
The volunteers assisted the elderly visitors in climbing the temple steps. (help) → The volunteers helped the elderly visitors climb / to climb the temple steps.
11.
The sudden hailstorm forced everyone to run for shelter. (make) → The sudden hailstorm made everyone run for shelter.
12.
Her grandmother always allowed her to choose the tea bowl herself. (let) → Her grandmother always let her choose the tea bowl herself.
13.
The manager arranged for the broken window to be fixed before the guests arrived. (have — passive causative) → The manager had the broken window fixed before the guests arrived.
Bonus pattern: have + object + past participle — used when someone else does the action for you (like "get a haircut"). Different from "have + object + base verb" (item 3), where you're arranging someone's action, not receiving a service.
14.
She managed, after two calls, to persuade the airline to change her seat. (get) → She got, after two calls, the airline to change her seat.
15.
The kind neighbour assisted the tourists in finding their way back to the station. (help) → The kind neighbour helped the tourists find / to find their way back to the station.
