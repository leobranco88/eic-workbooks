01Before You Write · Part Areference

Two things to check, every time: the tense cue, and the phrase that becomes your new subject.

Cue wordsUseMeaning
whenever / always / every timemakes / makea habit, a repeated pattern
since / already / recentlyhas made / have madea change that is still true now
a specific past moment (yesterday, last night, in 2019)madeone completed event, finished
next time / from tomorrow / if… willwill makea prediction, something not yet true
worked example
Because of the constant rain, the streets have been dangerous all week.
1. Tense cue: "have been… all week" → present perfect → has made
2. Subject-to-be: "the constant rain" — the cause, moved to the front
3. Object + adjective: the streets / dangerous
→ The constant rain has made the streets dangerous.
tense cuebecomes the subject

Rewrite each sentence as: subject + make + object + adjective. Underlined in red is your tense cue; underlined in teal is what becomes your subject.

01

SuggestedThe rain at night makes me sleepy.
02

SuggestedOur Sunday calls make her happier.
03
Every time there's silence in the office after six, I feel calmer.
SuggestedThe silence in the office after six makes me calmer.
04
Since the new project, my colleagues have been more stressed.
SuggestedThe new project has made them more stressed.
05
My father has been healthier lately, because of his diet.
SuggestedHis diet has made him healthier.
06
My sister has already felt more confident, since her promotion.
SuggestedHer promotion has already made her more confident."Already" sits between has and the main verb: has already made.
07
Last night, because of the strange noise, I felt nervous.
SuggestedThe strange noise made me nervous last night.
08
In January, after his terrible flu, my brother felt weak for weeks.
SuggestedHis terrible flu made him weak for weeks.
09
Because of the sudden storm, we were anxious for hours yesterday.
SuggestedThe sudden storm made us anxious for hours yesterday.
10
From tomorrow, because of the new schedule, the team will feel rushed.
SuggestedThe new schedule will make the team rushed.Will make never changes form — no -s, whatever the subject.
11
Next time, because of the traffic, I'll probably be late again.
SuggestedThe traffic will make me late again.
12
Because of the long meeting, my colleagues will be irritated by lunchtime.
SuggestedThe long meeting will make them irritated by lunchtime.
calm or calmer? Both are correct — they say different things. It makes me calm describes the state she ends in. It makes me calmer describes a change of degree: she was already a little calm, and moved further. Choose on purpose, not by accident.
03Before You Write · Part Breference

Sometimes the cause isn't a simple noun — it's an action. Turn the whole clause into the subject.

worked example
My neighbour plays the piano at midnight.
1. Find the verb: plays
2. Remove the tense, add -ing: playing
3. The whole clause becomes the subject: My neighbour playing the piano at midnight
4. Add make + object + adjective
→ My neighbour playing the piano at midnight makes me angry.
The tense doesn't disappear — it moves onto make: plays (present) → makes · played (past) → made. This is the same shape as the participle clauses you already know — a clause with no tense of its own, working as a noun.
the whole clause becomes the subjectthe verb that changes to -ing
