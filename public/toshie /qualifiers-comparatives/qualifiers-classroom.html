How much better?

Comparativos como "better" ou "more expensive" dizem que algo é diferente. Mas o inglês tem um jeito de dizer o tamanho dessa diferença: enorme (way better), pequena (slightly better), igual (just as good as) ou nenhuma vantagem (not as good as). Hoje aprendemos a medir a diferença.

01
Kyoto or Osaka?
Leitura · By Yuki Tanaka, travel essayist
A short essay on choosing between two cities

People always ask me which city I prefer, Kyoto or Osaka. Honestly, it depends on what you want. Kyoto is way quieter than Osaka. The temples, the gardens, the narrow streets all feel calmer. If you want peace, Kyoto wins easily.

Osaka, though, is a lot more fun at night. The food scene is way better, in my opinion, and the people are much more relaxed with strangers. Kyoto can feel a little more formal sometimes, almost too polite.

Prices are not as different as people think. Osaka is only slightly cheaper for food, and hotels in both cities cost roughly the same. If price is your main worry, the city itself won't change much.

What surprised me most was the trains. Kyoto's local buses are nowhere near as convenient as Osaka's subway system. In Osaka, everything connects. In Kyoto, you often wait, transfer, and wait again.

In the end, Kyoto is just as beautiful as everyone says, but Osaka is far friendlier for a short, easy trip. Choose based on what kind of exhausted you want to feel by the end of the day: peacefully tired, or happily worn out.

02
Medindo a diferença
Qualifiers que intensificam ou suavizam o comparativo
Diferença enorme
way / far / a lot / much
+ comparativo

A diferença é grande, óbvia, gritante.

"This hotel is way more expensive than that one."

Diferença pequena
slightly / a bit / a little
+ comparativo

A diferença existe, mas é pequena, quase imperceptível.

"The train is only slightly faster than the bus."

Sem diferença
just as ... as
+ adjetivo base

As duas coisas são iguais nesse aspecto.

"This restaurant is just as good as the other one."

Sem vantagem nenhuma
not as ... as / nowhere near as ... as
+ adjetivo base

Uma coisa fica claramente abaixo da outra.

"This hotel is nowhere near as comfortable as a ryokan."

A regra de ouro: "way" e "far" só combinam com a forma comparativa ("way better", "far more expensive"). Eles nunca aparecem dentro de uma estrutura "as...as". Não existe "way as good as", isso soa errado. Para intensificar uma estrutura de igualdade, use "just as...as".

Diferença enorme

One hotel room costs $40 a night. The other, right across the street, costs $400 a night.

This hotel is way more expensive than that one.

Por quê "way more expensive": a diferença de preço é enorme (10 vezes mais). "Way" e "far" combinam exatamente com esse tipo de diferença gritante.

Diferença pequena

One train takes 2 hours and 10 minutes. The other takes 2 hours and 15 minutes.

This train is only slightly faster than that one.

Por quê "slightly faster": a diferença é de apenas 5 minutos, quase nada. "Slightly", "a bit" e "a little" são para diferenças pequenas assim.
