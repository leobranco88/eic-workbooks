Was it happening?

Uma extensão da dedução no passado: em vez de "aconteceu e terminou", às vezes você quer dizer "estava acontecendo naquele momento". Para isso, o inglês usa modal + have been + verbo-ing.

01The Room Above Mine
Leitura · By Yuki Tanaka
A small hotel · somewhere near Kyoto · 2am

Something above my room kept making noise all night. Feet moving. A chair scraping. A door closing softly. It was 2am. Someone up there must have been walking around, back and forth, unable to sleep either.

In the morning, I asked the front desk about it. The receptionist looked confused. "That room is empty," she said. "No one has stayed there this week." The noise can't have been coming from the hallway either. I could hear it directly above me, not beside me.

I thought about it on the train home. It might have been the pipes, settling as the building cooled down. Or the sound might have been coming from a completely different room, one floor away, echoing strangely through the old walls. I'll never know for certain. But something, somewhere, must have been happening.

02A forma contínua
Modal + have been + verbo-ing
~95%
must have been + -ing

Quase certeza de que algo estava em andamento.

"The lights were on. Someone must have been cooking."

~50%
might/could have been + -ing

Possibilidade de uma ação em progresso no passado.

"She might have been sleeping when you called."

~50%
may have been + -ing

Igual a might have been, mais formal.

"They may have been waiting outside."

~95%
can't have been + -ing

Praticamente impossível que a ação estivesse ocorrendo.

"He can't have been driving that fast here."

A ideia central: a forma simples ("must have missed") descreve uma ação completa. A forma contínua ("must have been missing" — quando faz sentido) descreve uma ação em progresso num momento específico do passado. É a mesma lógica do Present Continuous vs. Present Simple, só que aplicada à dedução.
ContextoSimples (ação completa)Contínua (em andamento)
Panelas ainda na cozinha, cheiro de comidaSomeone must have cooked dinner.Someone must have been cooking when you arrived.
Cabelo molhado, respiração ofeganteShe must have exercised.She must have been exercising right before you called.
Barreiras de concreto tornam a velocidade impossívelA car can't have driven that fast.A car can't have been driving that fast here.
Como escolher
Pistas de contexto que pedem a forma contínua: "when I arrived", "at that moment", "still", "for two hours", "on and off". Pistas que pedem a forma simples: "already", "by the time", evidência de um resultado finalizado (prato pronto, e-mail respondido, tarefa concluída).
Simples vs. contínua

You come home and find your roommate's laptop open, a half-written email on the screen, and coffee still warm beside it.

She must have been answering emails when something interrupted her.

Por quê a forma contínua: o e-mail está pela metade e o café ainda está quente. As evidências apontam para uma ação interrompida, em andamento, não uma ação já concluída.

Impossibilidade contínua

Your friend says he was watching TV all evening, but the remote's batteries died three days ago and were never replaced.

He can't have been watching TV all evening. There's no way to turn it on.

Por quê "can't have been watching": a evidência (pilhas mortas) torna fisicamente impossível a ação contínua alegada.
