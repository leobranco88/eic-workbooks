EXTRA Who / What / Where — Now With Mixed Tenses 20 items · is or has?

Same rule as before: a dependent preposition connects a verb or adjective to a person, a thing, or a place — so every question uses who, what, or where. This time the sentences use different tenses, and some use the contraction 's or 'd. Before you can build the question, you need to work out what the contraction actually means.

Example 1 — 's = has
She's arrived at a final decision about the refund.
Think: "arrived" is a past participle, so 's = has (present perfect) — "she has arrived." "A final decision" is a thing, so the question starts with What.
→ What has she arrived at?
Example 2 — 's = is
She's very proud of how she handled it.
Think: "proud" is an adjective, not a past participle, so 's = is — "she is proud." The question keeps the same tense: is, not has.
→ What is she proud of?
