Strategic HR · Pay talk

Market,
not need.

In English, pay talk runs on data and soft verbs. Nobody says I want a raise.

You ask to talk, you bring the market, and you close with a question. HR reads your request the same way from the other side of the table, and this page shows both sides. Everything here is also on the Cheat sheet, ready to print.

Stay, or run?

Before you watch

Talk about these first. Then watch and see if Vivian agrees with you.

  1. When was the last time someone on your team asked for a raise? How did it go?
  2. What's the strongest argument an employee can bring to a pay conversation?
  3. How do you find out what the market pays for a role?
  4. If a great employee told you she had a better offer, what would you do first?
  5. Should a company ever match an offer from a competitor? Why or why not?
  6. Is looking for another job while you're still employed disloyal?

3 The ask

Four moves, in this order.

The marked part is the chunk. Keep it, and change the rest to fit your story.

1
Book the time
Never in the hallway. Put it on the calendar.
I'd like to schedule some time to discuss my compensation.
2
Bring the market
Start with data. It's the argument HR respects.
Based on the research I've done, my salary is a bit below market for this role.
3
Show the value
What has changed in your work since your last raise.
Since my last salary adjustment, I've taken on the whole onboarding process.
4
Name it, then ask
Give a range, and end with a question, not a demand.
I'd like to discuss a market adjustment of 8 to 10 percent. Does that sound fair?
Too blunt → Say this
I want a raise.→I'd like to discuss my compensation.
My rent went up.→My role has grown since my last review.
I've been here three years, so I deserve it.→Here's what I've delivered this year.
If you don't pay me more, I'll leave.→I'm committed here, and I'd like my pay to reflect the market.
4 The answer

What comes back.

When they say no.

A no is usually a not now. Ask for the criteria and a date.
Ask for the criteria
What would it take for me to be considered for a raise this year?
Ask for a date
Is there a better time for us to revisit this?
Move to something else
Is there any flexibility on vacation time? Is that something you'd be open to?

When there's another offer.

Tell your manager first, and never as a threat.
To your manager
I wanted to let you know that I've been offered a position as HR Manager with another company.
Leave the door open
I'm open to discussing how my current role could evolve.
Saying no to a counteroffer
Your offer was generous, but the new role gives me the growth I want to prioritize.
Saying yes
After careful consideration, I've decided to stay.
5 The band

Read the band.

HR doesn't say cheap or expensive. It says where you sit. The numbers under the band are the compa-ratio.

TermHow it sounds · in a meeting
Pay band
The pay range for a role: minimum, midpoint, maximum.
She's sitting at the bottom of the band.
Compa-ratio
Salary divided by the midpoint. 1.00 is right in the middle.
He's at a compa-ratio of 0.88.
Market adjustment
A raise because the market moved, outside the review cycle.
This is a market adjustment, not a merit increase.
Merit increase
A raise for performance, in the yearly cycle.
Merit increases are tied to the review cycle.
Pay compression
New hires earn almost the same as people who've been there for years.
Matching that offer would create compression.
Internal equity
Fair pay between roles inside the company.
A 30% bump would break internal equity.
Total comp
Salary plus bonus, benefits and stock.
Let's look at total comp, not just base.
Flight risk
Someone with a real chance of leaving.
She's a flight risk, and she'd be a regrettable loss.
6 Stay

Five questions before they leave.

Ask these before the resignation letter, not after. The manager runs it, and the one rule is: don't get defensive.

  1. What do you look forward to each day when you commute to work?
  2. What are you learning here, and what do you want to learn?
  3. Why do you stay here?
  4. When is the last time you thought about leaving us, and what prompted it?
  5. What can I do to make your job better for you?

Watch out. False friends.
