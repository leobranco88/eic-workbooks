Sell the credit.

Brazil is changing how it taxes what you sell. In B2B, your buyer will not compare prices. He will compare what each offer really costs after the tax credit. This lesson gives you the system, the words, and the sentences for that conversation.

2Part 2 of 13

Start here: tax from zero

New to the subject? Start here. You do not need to know anything about tax.

  1. 1

    A tax on the price

    When you buy something, a part of the price goes to the government. That part is the tax. Brazil has had five of these taxes on consumption. The reform replaces them with two.

  2. 2

    Tax on the value added

    A product passes through many companies before it reaches the customer. In a value added tax, each company pays tax only on what it adds. It charges tax on its sale, and then it takes away the tax it already paid on its purchase. That subtraction is the tax credit. Follow the money:

Maker–R$ 100R$ 10R$ 0R$ 10

Invented numbers, for practice. Tax rate: 10%. Prices are before tax. The final customer pays R$ 220 and takes no credit.

Look at the totals: the government receives R$ 20, exactly 10% of the final price before tax. Nobody pays tax on tax, because each company takes back what it already paid.

  1. 3

    Why a seller cares

    Your buyer is a company, not a final customer. The tax on your invoice comes back to your buyer as credit, so the buyer looks at the net price, not at the total. This is why this lesson is about selling the credit.

The system, before and after

For decades, Brazil taxed consumption with five taxes, each with its own rules. The new system replaces them with two taxes that work like a value added tax. It is already in law. What is not final yet is the tax rate.

The road to 2033

  1. 2026Test year. CBS 0.9% and IBS 0.1% are shown on invoices. No extra payment. All the old taxes stay.
  2. 2027CBS in full. PIS and Cofins end. IPI goes to zero. The selective tax starts.
  3. 2029 to 2032ICMS and ISS fall by a tenth a year. IBS rises to replace them.
  4. 2033The new system is complete. ICMS and ISS are gone.

6Part 6 of 13

The conversation, step by step

Five steps for any talk with a buyer. Learn the sentences in bold.

  1. 1Name the change early.

    “From 2027, the tax on our invoice works differently.”

    Say it before the buyer asks. You look prepared, not defensive.

  2. 2Show the invoice in two lines.

    “This is the price. The tax is shown on top, as a separate line.”

    Two numbers, two lines. Never mix them.

  3. 3Compare the net cost.

    “Let’s compare the net cost: what each offer costs after the credit.”

    The buyer’s finance team already thinks this way. Join them.

  4. 4Check the credit.

    “Can your company take the full credit on this purchase?”

    Ask. Never promise. The credit depends on the buyer’s regime.

  5. 5Put it in writing.

    “I’ll send the quote with the tax on a separate line.”

    A written quote ends the doubt and starts the decision.

7Part 7 of 13

Too blunt → Say this

Each sentence on the left is true, but it hurts the sale. Say a better version out loud first. Then check.

  • “I don’t understand the new taxes.”→“I’ll confirm that with our finance team and get back to you today.”
  • “Just trust me, you’ll get the credit.”→“Your finance team can confirm the credit. I’ll send you the details.”
  • “We can’t lower the price.”→“Let’s look at the net cost first, and then at the price.”
  • “The tax is not my problem.”→“Let me check how this affects your credit.”
8Part 8 of 13

When the buyer pushes back

Five things buyers say, and answers you can use.

  • “Your total is higher than the other quote.”“The total includes tax you can recover. Can we compare the price before tax?”
  • “The other supplier is in Simples Nacional.”“The credit can be smaller with a Simples supplier. It’s worth checking the number with your finance team.”
  • “Can you absorb the tax?”“I can’t absorb it, but I can look at the volume, the terms or the delivery schedule.”
  • “Is our contract still safe?”“Let’s add a tax review clause: the price is reviewed if the tax changes.”
  • “How much is the tax going to be?”“The final rate is still being defined. I’ll send the figure as soon as finance confirms it.”

One rule: Do not guess. When you do not know, say you will confirm. Never quote a final tax rate.

The credit check, and your turn

  2. 2“Can you take the full credit on this purchase?”
  3. 3“Does finance compare quotes before or after tax?”
  4. 4“Do you need the tax on separate lines on the quote?”

Your turn

The buyer says: “Your total is higher than the other quote.” Answer in two sentences.

“The total includes tax you can recover as credit. Can we compare the price before tax?”

Your turn

The buyer asks: “Can you absorb the tax?” Say no, and offer something else.

“I can’t absorb it, but I can look at the volume, the terms or the delivery schedule.”

Your turn

A new colleague asks: What changes on the invoice from 2027? Explain in three sentences.

“The new federal tax, CBS, is collected in full from 2027, and PIS and Cofins end. The tax is shown on top of the price, as a separate line. Buyers can usually recover it as credit, so we compare the net cost, not the total.”
