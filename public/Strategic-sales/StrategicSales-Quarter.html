Plan it. Date it.

A good quarter plan turns a vague feeling into a number, a risk and a date. The same skill works on a slow month and on a customer who says "not this quarter."

    3The plan

    Present your quarter in five moves

    Say the number first. Separate what is confirmed from what you hope. Put an action on every risk and a date on every ask.

    1. 1

      Let me walk you through my Q4 plan, and start with the number.

      "I'm at 62% of quota with five weeks left. Here is where I need you."

    2. 2

      Separate what is confirmed from what you hope: is it a commit or best case?

      "It's best case for now. Legal hasn't signed off yet."

    3. 3

      I want to flag one risk early. Attach an action to every risk.

      "It's not in danger yet, but I'm watching it. I'll confirm the review date this week."

    4. 4

      Name the pattern, then the play: "Q3 is typically soft for us, so I'm planning to..."

      "...reach out to 20 dormant accounts this month and book renewal talks early."

    5. 5

      Ask for support, with a date: "Where I could use your help is..."

      "...the call with their CFO. Could you join on the 14th?"

    Too blunt, say this

    Say the better version out loud first. Then tap to check.

    4The answer

    When the customer says "Let's talk next quarter"

    Do not argue. Accept it, ask what changes, and turn "next quarter" into a week.

    1. 1

      Accept, then ask what changes.

      "Understood, and thanks for being upfront. What changes between now and then?"

      A concrete answer ("our budget resets in January") means a real deal. A vague answer is often a soft no.

    2. 2

      Make the cost of waiting visible, gently.

      "I'm not trying to rush you. I just want us to weigh what waiting would cost."

    3. 3

      Turn "next quarter" into a week.

      "Could we get 20 minutes with legal in the next two weeks? I'll check in the week of January 12."

    4. 4

      Lower the risk.

      "We can start small with a pilot, so there's less risk on your side."

    5. 5

      Stay in touch without pestering.

      "Would you be open to me sending one useful resource in the meantime? No pitch."

    A real exchange

    Customer: This looks good, but let's talk next quarter.

    You: Understood, thanks for being upfront. Help me understand: what changes between now and next quarter?

    Customer: Our budget resets in January, and I need legal to look at it.

    You: That's helpful. Let's put together a short mutual action plan with owners and dates. Could we get 20 minutes with legal in the next two weeks? I'll check in the week of January 12.

    When your manager challenges you

    Manager: Is Acme really a commit, or are we hoping legal moves faster?

    You: Fair question. The economic buyer confirmed budget, but legal hasn't started. I'll move it to best case until I have a review date. Can you help me get their legal team to prioritize it?

    Manager: Isn't that just an excuse? (about a seasonal dip)

    You: I hear you. That's why I'm not waiting for leads to come in. What would you want to see from me to feel comfortable with the plan?

    Your turn

    The customer says: "We love it, but our budget is frozen until January." Answer in three moves: accept, ask, date.

    "Understood, thanks for telling me. When does the budget reopen, and who signs off? Could we put a 20-minute call on the calendar for the week of January 12, and I'll send a short pilot outline in the meantime?"
    5Push or wait?

    Close it this quarter, or protect the relationship?

    This is the debate behind question 6. Niemiec says to prepare early and build the plan with the customer. Brock says many close dates are the seller's choice, not the customer's. They mostly agree. The real question is what to do when the date is close. Here is the case for each side.

    The case for pushing

    • A firm expiry date on a negotiated price can move a stalled deal.
    • Agree on the decision timeline early, in discovery.
    • Keep enough pipeline coverage, so quarter end is not do-or-die.

    The case for waiting

    • End-of-quarter discounts teach customers to wait for the next one.
    • Every discount makes each deal worth less.
    • A close date you chose is a weak signal. A date the customer gave you is a strong one.
    • Scaring the customer into acting gives them more to worry about.

    The middle path: a real deadline, linked to the customer's own situation, is fine. A manufactured deadline is not.

    Plan like a project manager

    Work backwards from the date you want. Then every step has an owner and a day.

    "If we want to sign by the 30th, legal needs the contract by the 15th. So the security review has to start this week."

    Three lines for the middle path

    1. 1

      Give an honest deadline.

      "Our current pricing is held through the 30th. After that, I'd have to re-quote. Would it help to decide by then, or should we plan around your timeline?"

    2. 2

      Use their date, not yours.

      "You said the budget closes on November 30. Let's work backwards from that date."

    3. 3

      Say no to a discount for timing.

      "I can't lower the price just for signing earlier. I can move on price if we move to a two-year term."

      This trades a concession for something in return.

    Your turn

    The customer says: "If you give me 15% off, I'll sign this quarter." Answer without giving the discount away.

    "I appreciate the clear offer. I can't discount just for timing, but let's see what we can trade. If we move to a two-year term, I can look at the price. Is the budget approved for this quarter, and who signs off?"
    Your opinion

    Is a deadline artificial if the discount really expires?

    "I think it depends on who owns the deadline. If the offer really expires and I tell the customer early, that is honest. If I invent a date to create pressure, it is not. The customer should always have enough time to decide."
    6The other side

    Read the quarter like your manager and your buyer

    Quarter end is a signal for you and leverage for the buyer. Know how the other side reads it.

    PipelineEarly stage, low confidence
    Best caseGood signals, open conditions
    CommitYour public promise
    ClosedSigned

    Coverage = qualified pipeline ÷ target. About 3x is the common baseline. Many B2B teams aim for 3x to 5x.

    If your win rate is 25%, you need about 4x. A deal that slips once can stay in the forecast. After a second push, take it out of commit.

    What the manager asks

    • "What would have to be true for this to close this quarter?"
    • "Is the close date yours or theirs?"
    • "Has legal, security or procurement started?"
    • "What's the next step, and is it on the calendar?"
    • "Who is the economic buyer?"

    What the buyer says

    • "We can sign by the 30th if the price reflects the volume."
    • "We have a competing quote, so I need you to sharpen your number."
    • "Procurement needs to review this first, so I can't commit to a date."
    • "That's outside our budget this year." (a budget freeze)

    Sales-floor talk: can you say it out loud?

    ExpressionMeaningAt work, say
    commitA deal you promise to close"I'm committing this deal."
    sandbaggingHiding a deal's potential to look better later"I'm being conservative on timing."
    happy earsHearing what you want to hear"I don't have written confirmation yet."
    kick the canKeep pushing a decision later"We're moving the decision to March."
    go darkA customer stops answering"We haven't heard back since the demo."
    pipe genTime for finding new deals"I'm blocking Tuesday mornings for pipe gen."

    Blue thumb: fine in a meeting. Pink thumb: common among colleagues, but not what you say about yourself or to a customer.

    Tool: the mutual action plan

    A shared list of steps, owners and dates, for both sides. It turns "we'll see" into a calendar.

    "Let's put together a short plan with owners and dates on both sides. Who else should be in the loop?"

    7Low season

    A slow period is a work period

    Do not coast, and do not panic. Name the pattern, show the plan, ask for what you need.

    ChunkIn a sentence
    seasonal dip"This is a seasonal dip, not a trend. Same pattern as last year."
    dormant accounts"I'm re-engaging dormant accounts from the last 12 months."
    replenish the pipeline"I want to replenish the pipeline before the fall rush."
    off-peak"We're in off-peak season, so we have room to plan."
    use-it-or-lose-it"They have use-it-or-lose-it money, so the window closes November 30."
    sales cycle"Deals started now won't close until Q4, given our 90-day sales cycle."
    trade, don't give"I can move on price if we move to a two-year term."
    hold a price"I can hold this price until September 30."

    Explaining a dip to your manager

    "Q3 is typically soft for us, and this dip lines up with last year, so I don't think it's a performance issue. My plan has three parts. First, I'll reach out to 20 dormant accounts this month. Second, I'm booking renewal conversations early. Third, I'll spend Fridays cleaning up the CRM. I expect closings to be light until mid-September, and I'll update you every two weeks. Could I get some time with marketing on a re-engagement email?"

    Sample answer. The pattern: name the dip with history, give a numbered plan, quantify, ask for support.

    Your turn

    Your manager asks: "Isn't a slow month just a good excuse to relax?" Answer with a pattern, a plan and an ask.

    "I understand the concern. This month is soft every year, so I'm using it for work I can't do in a busy month: re-engaging dormant accounts, booking renewals early and building pipeline for the fall. I'll send you the list on Friday. Could you introduce me to two accounts from your network?"
    8Glossary

    Watch out, then look it up

    False friends

    WordFrom the PortugueseWhat it really means
    eventuallyeventualmenteIt means "in the end." For "sometimes" or "maybe," say possibly or occasionally.
    actuallyatualmenteIt means "in fact." For "now," say currently.
    pretendpretenderIt means "to act as if." For "to plan," say intend or plan.
    attend a clientatender um clienteIt means "to go to." For "to help a client," say serve or help.

    Also: say "fall," not "autumn," in the US. Say "pipeline," not "funnel," for deals in progress. "Off-peak" or "slow season" sounds more natural than "low season."
