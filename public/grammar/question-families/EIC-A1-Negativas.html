Antes de começar

As 3 famílias de tempo verbal

O segredo da negativa é sempre o mesmo: tudo depende de já existir ou não um auxiliar na frase. Veja como isso muda no presente simples, no passado e no present continuous.

Família A · Presente

Don't / Doesn't

have to · want to · need to · try to · plan to
No presente, a frase não tem auxiliar próprio. Para negar, inserimos don't ou doesn't antes do verbo. Depende de quem é o sujeito.
Afirm. Felipe wants to change jobs.
→ Felipe doesn't want to change jobs.
Família B · Passado

Didn't

had to · wanted to · needed to · tried to · planned to
No passado, o auxiliar é sempre didn't. Não importa o sujeito. O verbo principal volta para a forma base (sem -ed) na negativa.
Afirm. Rosana wanted to save money.
→ Rosana didn't want to save money.
Família C · Continuous

Isn't / Aren't

is/are + verb-ing
No present continuous, o auxiliar (is/are) já está na frase. Para negar, não inserimos nada novo. Apenas adicionamos not logo depois.
Afirm. Felipe is working at the site.
→ Felipe isn't working at the site.
Família A · em detalhe

Com quem usamos cada um?

O auxiliar muda dependendo do sujeito da frase. Veja exatamente quem usa don't e quem usa doesn't.

don't
I you we they
Rosana and Felipe don't try to eat healthy.
doesn't
he she it
Felipe doesn't want to change jobs.
Brazilian Trap · The most common mistakes
She doesn't wants to change jobs.
→ She doesn't want to change jobs.
After doesn't, the verb goes back to its base form.
She didn't wanted to save money.
→ She didn't want to save money.
After didn't, the verb goes back to its base form.
She don't have to work late.
→ She doesn't have to work late.
she/he/it always take doesn't, not don't.
