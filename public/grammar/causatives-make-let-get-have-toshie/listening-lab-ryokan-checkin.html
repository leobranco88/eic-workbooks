The Ryokan That Wouldn't Let Us In

A short listening piece about a hotel, a long wait, and the five causative verbs — make, let, get, have, help. Don't read anything yet. Just press play.

Before you listen
  • Before you listen: guess. Why might a guest have to wait two hours before checking into a room?
  • You will hear this twice. The first time, just listen for the general story. The second time, listen for detail.
Press play. Listen once for the story — don't write anything yet.

You can press play again any time. Nothing is written on this page until you open "Show transcript" at the very end.

Task 1 · First Listen

Listen once. Answer in your own words — short answers are fine.

1. Why couldn't the guests check in right away?

2. What did the owner do instead of letting them into the room immediately?

3. How did the guest feel about the wait, by the end of the story?

Task 2 · Second Listen

Listen again, more carefully this time. True or False?

1. True or False: The narrator arrived exactly at check-in time.

They arrived two hours early.

2. True or False: The owner refused to help them at all while they waited.

She got them to leave their bags, made tea, and helped them plan the afternoon.

3. True or False: The room had a view of the garden.

4. True or False: The owner let them into the room as soon as they arrived.

She let them in at four o'clock.

5. True or False: The narrator ends the story frustrated about the wait.

The wait taught them something about patience.

Structure Spotlight

The same five causative verbs from the reading lab. Same idea, five different meanings.

CaseStructureMeaning
MAKEmake + object + base verb / adjectiveforce — no choice
LETlet + object + base verbpermission — full choice
GETget + object + to + verbpersuade — takes effort
HAVEhave + object + base verbarrange — it's their role
HELPhelp + object + (to) + verbassist — together

Discussion

No right answer. Use the target verbs if you can.

Have you ever been made to wait somewhere and ended up not minding it? What happened?

Would you rather stay somewhere with strict check-in times, or somewhere more flexible? Why?

What's one thing a hotel or host has done for you that felt like more than just following the rules?

Transcript

Only open this after you've finished both tasks.

We arrived at the ryokan two hours before check-in, exhausted from the train. The owner didn't let us into our room, but she got us to leave our bags at the front desk, and made us feel welcome anyway. She had the kitchen prepare tea immediately, and she helped us plan the afternoon so the wait wouldn't feel wasted. By four o'clock, she finally let us in. The room had tatami floors and a view of the garden. That evening, the ryokan made me understand something about patience I hadn't expected from a hotel stay: sometimes being made to wait is the whole point.
