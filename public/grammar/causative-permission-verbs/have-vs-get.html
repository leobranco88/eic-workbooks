The Sixth Verb

The Calm Cousin

MAKE forces. GET persuades. LET and ALLOW permit. There's a sixth verb that does none of that: HAVE. No pressure, no negotiation, just delegation. And it sits close enough to GET that the two get mixed up constantly.

Why this page exists
"I had my assistant book the flight" and "I got my assistant to book the flight" look almost identical. But one describes a normal Tuesday, and the other describes a small negotiation. Mix them up, and you accidentally imply your assistant didn't want to do their job.
01

Have someone do

HAVE + person + BASE VERB

Delegation, not force. You're asking for something that's already part of the person's role. Nobody needs to be convinced, and nobody's being obligated against their will.

Monday morning at the agency. Nobody negotiates who does what, the org chart already answered that question weeks ago.

Real talk Sem "to", igual make e let. Mas o peso é bem mais leve: você não está forçando nem convencendo, só delegando algo que já é papel da pessoa. "Have him do it" soa neutro, quase administrativo, bem diferente de "make him do it" (obrigação) ou "get him to do it" (esforço de persuasão).

02

Have vs Get, the mix-up

Same shop, same mechanic, two completely different relationships depending on which verb you choose.

Same photo, two different stories
Have, routine

"I have my mechanic check the brakes every six months."

Already his job. No convincing needed, it's scheduled maintenance.

Get, persuasion

"I got the mechanic to fix it for free after the warranty expired."

Not something he'd normally do. Someone had to ask, explain, negotiate.

Have

"The teacher has the students grade each other's homework."

Routine classroom task, built into the role.

Get

"She got the shy student to speak up in front of the class."

Took effort, encouragement, maybe several tries.

Real talk Repara na gramática antes mesmo do sentido: HAVE não tem "to" (igual MAKE e LET), GET tem "to" (igual ALLOW). Isso não é coincidência. Verbos sem "to" descrevem algo direto, sem processo no meio. Verbos com "to" descrevem um caminho até lá, seja força (MAKE, sem "to", exceção à parte) seja persuasão (GET) seja autorização formal (ALLOW).

Mixed review

Have, get, or something else?

Six of the family's verbs are in play now. Read carefully.

1. "The salon always ___ the same stylist cut my hair, she knows exactly how I like it."

2. "It wasn't easy, but we finally ___ the supplier to lower the price."

3. "The school doesn't ___ students bring phones into the exam room."

4. "My grandmother always ___ me stay for one more slice of cake."

5. "Every Friday, the manager ___ the junior staff prepare the weekly report, it's in their job description."
