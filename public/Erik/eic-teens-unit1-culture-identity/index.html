Four ways
to ask

You are going to interview four people here at school. Same subject, four different questions. Build them before you leave this room.

Before you ask

Excuse me, could I ask you four questions for my English class?

and at the end: Thank you very much!

Never ask an adult how old they are. Ask about the time instead: when you were my age …

Critical thinking

Show me
the proof

Writers don't just say things. They prove them. Open your book at the article and find the proof behind each claim.

1

What kind of proof?

Read the claim. Find it in the paragraph. Choose the kind of proof the writer uses, then check. The answer shows you the exact line: count from the first line of the paragraph.

BGen Z around the world have a lot in common.
B · line 4An example from the world. The writer compares Gen Z in Asia with Gen Z in Europe and North America, and opens the example with For example.
COlder generations grew up while everything was changing.
C · line 5An example from the world. The change from analog to digital communication. There is no linking expression here: the example just comes in the next sentence.
CPictures are a better way to share your life with friends.
C · lines 10–11A person's words. A 21-year-old from Switzerland explains it in a quote.
CGen Z like to experiment with the way they look.
C · line 19, right columnA number. Almost half of them say so.
DLife in the real world is different for Gen Z, too.
D · lines 3–4A number. About 22 percent have at least one immigrant parent, introduced with For instance. Remember this one for section 3.
DGen Z feel confident about who they are.
D · line 6A person's words. A 21-year-old from the Netherlands. Remember this one for section 3, too.
EA global identity is a good thing.
E · lines 7–8No proof. The claim is the very last sentence. The writer says it, even asks you to agree, but never shows you why. Section 4 is where you fix that.
2

The door to the example

Some expressions open the door to an example. Find these four in the article. Select only the ones that introduce an example, then check.

For example B · line 4such as C · line 4For instance D · line 3
These three open the door to an example. in other words C · line 2is the trap: it says the same idea again in a simpler way. It explains. It doesn't prove.
3

How strong is it?

Good proof survives two questions. Ask them about each one.

How many people?
Where?

C · line 995 percent of Gen Z communicate with pictures and videos.

Strong. How many people? Almost everybody. That is hard to argue with.

D · line 4About 22 percent of Gen Z have an immigrant parent.

Be careful. Where? Only in the United States. It may be true there and not true in Brazil, so it can't prove something about Gen Z everywhere.

D · line 6A 21-year-old from the Netherlands says people are more themselves now.

Be careful. How many people? One. It is a real voice, and real voices are good proof, but one voice is not a whole generation.
4

Paragraph E needs you

Paragraph E makes a claim and never proves it. Use something a real person told you in your interviews and give the writer the example that is missing.

E · lines 4–5
A global identity can bring people together.

Start with For example or For instance.

One possible answer. For example, my teacher and my cousin are from different generations, but they both use WhatsApp to stay in touch with family in other countries.

Real Talk

Afirmação sem prova é só opinião. Toda vez que alguém disser que algo é verdade, faça duas perguntas: quantas pessoas? onde? Se a resposta for "uma pessoa" ou "só num país", a prova existe, mas é fraca. E a entrevista que você fez no corredor é exatamente o mesmo tipo de prova que o livro usa: a voz de uma pessoa real.
