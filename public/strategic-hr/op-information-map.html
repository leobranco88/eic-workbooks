Strategic HR · The Information Map

Not Just a Filter. A Source.

You already know how each seat filters what it hears. Now go one step further: each seat also holds information nobody else has, and each one hands it over differently, some straight out, some between the lines. Reading both is what makes you useful to Renata, not just informed.

Before You Start · Continuing the Coaching

This picks up right where Navigating Power left off. You already learned that the CEO, CFO, COO, CRO, and CHRO each filter the same message differently. This lesson adds two more layers: how each of them actually talks (direct or indirect), and what unique information only they can give you. Renata still needs your read on both, especially with the CRO and the CHRO, the two she finds hardest to get a straight answer from.

Think about it
The CRO and the CHRO are both indirect, but for very different reasons. What might explain each one?
Model answer

The CRO is often indirect out of caution: a firm answer today can become a liability if the situation changes tomorrow, so hedging protects the bank as much as it protects them. The CHRO is often indirect out of care: building context and reading the room first respects the fact that people, not numbers, are usually involved.

Think about it
Why is it useful to think of a stakeholder as a source of information, not only as someone who approves or blocks a decision?
Model answer

Treating someone only as a gate means you only talk to them when you need something. Treating them as a source means you talk to them earlier, and what they tell you can change the proposal itself, before it ever needs their approval.

03The Language

Eight expressions for talking about how people deliver information, straight out, carefully, or somewhere in between. Hover the highlighted phrase for the translation, then make your call.

1

Filling in for a meeting, the CRO's deputy tells Renata directly: "This plan is too risky. I wouldn't approve it as it is." No hedging, no cushioning. Renata isn't used to hearing no this clearly at Valore Bank. He's simply direto ao ponto, sem rodeiosDizer algo de forma muito direta e honesta, mesmo que seja desconfortável de ouvir, sem suavizar as palavras.Other ways to say this: straightforward, frank, plain-spoken.

Think about it
What is the advantage of a blunt answer, even when it's uncomfortable to hear?
Model answer

A blunt answer removes doubt about where someone stands, so you can act on it right away instead of guessing what they really meant.

Your intervention
Solid moveA blunt no is still information. Finding out exactly what would need to change turns a hard sentence into a usable next step.
RiskyArguing back treats honesty as an attack instead of a signal worth exploring, and it can make the next blunt comment harder to get.
RiskyAvoiding the person who tells the truth means losing access to exactly the kind of clarity Renata needs most.
2

When Renata asks the CHRO directly whether a reorg will hurt morale, he doesn't say yes or no. He says: "There will be an adjustment period, and how we communicate it will matter a lot." He's being diplomático, cuidadoso ao falarEscolher as palavras com cuidado para transmitir algo delicado sem ofender ou causar alarme desnecessário, mas ainda dizendo a verdade.Other ways to say this: tactful, careful with words, measured, not avoiding the topic.

Think about it
What is the difference between being diplomatic and simply avoiding an honest answer?
Model answer

Diplomatic language still delivers the real information, just wrapped carefully. Avoiding an answer gives no information at all. The CHRO already told her morale will be affected.

Your intervention
Solid moveThe answer is already there, just carefully worded. Helping Renata hear it saves her from missing information that was never actually hidden.
RiskyDismissing a diplomatic answer as empty means losing real information because it wasn't delivered bluntly.
RiskyDemanding a blunt yes or no from someone who communicates carefully can make them share even less next time.
3

In a private one-on-one, the CFO tells Renata something she'd never say in committee: "Honestly, this budget won't survive the next round of cuts." That kind of franqueza, sinceridadeHonestidade direta e sincera ao falar, geralmente reservada para conversas privadas onde a pessoa se sente segura pra dizer o que realmente pensa.Other ways to say this: frankness, honesty, openness rarely shows up in the room.

Think about it
Why might someone save their most candid opinion for a private conversation instead of the group meeting?
Model answer

Speaking candidly in front of a group carries more risk, being quoted or held to it later, so many people save their honest opinion for a setting where it's safer to share.

Your intervention
Solid moveUsing the information without exposing where it came from protects the CFO's trust and still lets Renata plan around the real picture.
RiskyRepeating a private comment publicly breaks trust and makes it far less likely anyone shares something candid with Renata again.
RiskyDismissing candid information just because it wasn't said officially throws away exactly the kind of insight that's hardest to get.
4

Renata needs to tell a longtime colleague his proposal was rejected. Instead of a blunt no, she says: "The direction was strong, but the timing doesn't fit this year's priorities. Let's revisit it next cycle." She's being sensível, com jeitoCuidadoso ao lidar com uma situação delicada ou com os sentimentos de alguém, sem deixar de comunicar a verdade.Other ways to say this: sensitive, considerate, gracious, not avoiding the news.

Think about it
What makes a tactful message different from one that just avoids the bad news entirely?
Model answer

A tactful message still delivers the real outcome, rejected for now, it just chooses a respectful way to say it. Avoiding the news would mean never delivering the outcome at all.

Your intervention
Solid moveThe message works because the outcome is still clear underneath the kindness. That's the real test of tact, not just softer wording.
RiskySoftening a message until the outcome disappears isn't tact anymore, it's just an unclear message that will need to be repeated later.
RiskyDropping the considerate framing entirely can damage a long relationship without making the message any more honest.
5

The CRO tells Renata: "I'd want to understand the full context before I comment on the exposure." She takes this as "he has no opinion yet." Her advisor points out she needs to ler nas entrelinhasEntender o significado real ou implícito de algo, além do que foi dito literalmente com as palavras.Other ways to say this: pick up on the subtext, sense the real meaning: he's signaling discomfort without saying it outright.

Think about it
What is lost if you only listen to the literal words someone says, without reading between the lines?
Model answer

You miss the real signal underneath. In this case, hesitation works like an early warning, and ignoring it means moving forward on something the person is quietly worried about.

Your intervention
Solid moveFollowing up privately gives him room to name the actual concern, turning a vague hesitation into something Renata can act on.
RiskyReading caution as approval ignores the exact signal Renata was coached to notice in the first place.
RiskyPressing him publicly for a clearer answer can make a cautious person retreat further instead of opening up.
6

Renata spends ten minutes in a meeting building context before mentioning an unpopular budget cut. Afterward, a colleague tells her: "You enrolar, dar voltasEvitar falar diretamente sobre um assunto difícil, adiando o ponto principal com conversa em volta dele.Other ways to say this: stall, avoid the point, dance around the issue, just tell them the number first next time."

Think about it
Why might someone beat around the bush before delivering unwelcome news?
Model answer

Delaying the hard part can feel like softening the blow, but it often makes the listener more anxious while they wait, and it can look like a lack of confidence in the message.

Your intervention
Solid moveLeading with the number removes the anxious wait, and the context still lands, just after the room knows what it's actually about.
RiskyMore context before the number just extends the wait, and it can read as avoiding the point rather than explaining it.
RiskySending hard news by email instead of addressing it directly avoids the delay, but it also avoids the conversation the room needs to have.
7

Renata's update to the CHRO says the team is "adjusting well" to a recent change. The truth is that two people are actively unhappy. She chose to suavizar, maquiar a verdadeApresentar uma notícia ruim de forma mais agradável do que a realidade, para evitar desconforto.Other ways to say this: soft-pedal, gloss over, put a positive spin on the update.

Think about it
What is the risk of sugarcoating a report to someone who could actually help fix the problem?
Model answer

The person who could help acts on a false picture instead. By the time the real problem surfaces, it's often harder and more expensive to fix than it would have been early.

Your intervention
Solid moveSharing the real picture now gives the CHRO, the one person equipped to help, the chance to act while the problem is still small.
RiskyCalling a softened report "mostly accurate" still leaves out the part that actually needs attention.
RiskyWaiting to see if an unhappy team improves on its own usually means the problem grows quietly instead.
8

Asked directly if a new process is safe, the CRO says: "It's probably fine, assuming nothing unusual happens." He's evitar se comprometer totalmenteEvitar dar uma resposta definitiva ou se comprometer totalmente com uma afirmação, geralmente para se proteger caso as coisas mudem.Other ways to say this: qualify a statement, avoid a firm commitment, protecting himself in case something does go wrong later.

Think about it
When is hedging a reasonable response, rather than someone avoiding responsibility?
Model answer

Hedging is reasonable when the real answer genuinely depends on conditions that haven't happened yet. It becomes avoidance only when someone hedges on facts they already know for certain.

Your intervention
Solid moveAsking what "unusual" actually means turns a vague hedge into a specific, usable answer, without demanding false certainty from him.
RiskyTreating a hedge as a firm yes ignores the exact caution he was signaling on purpose.
RiskyWriting him off as evasive closes a channel that, followed up correctly, still has real information in it.

05Your Reality

Reading style and reading information are two different skills. Together, they tell you not just what someone decided, but what they actually know.

Discussion 01

Why might an indirect communicator actually be giving you more information than a direct one, if you know how to listen for it?

Model answer

A direct communicator tells you what they think. An indirect one often tells you what they think, what they're worried about, and what they're not ready to commit to, all in the same careful sentence, if you know how to unpack it.

Discussion 02

What is the difference between adapting to someone's communication style and simply telling them what they want to hear?

Model answer

Adapting to a style changes how the message is delivered, direct or indirect, while the substance stays the same. Telling someone what they want to hear changes the substance itself, which is a different, more dangerous thing.

Discussion 03

Why does it matter to know what kind of information a stakeholder holds, before you even need anything from them?

Model answer

Waiting until you need something means you only ever ask questions shaped by that need. Knowing what someone holds in advance means you can ask better questions, earlier, before a decision is already half-made.
