Strategic HR · Performance Improvement Plans

The Paper Trail

A Performance Improvement Plan is supposed to be a genuine chance to improve. Too often, it's used as a slow, deniable way to build a case for firing someone. The difference between the two isn't intention, it's documentation. This lesson is about knowing which one you're looking at, and what to do either way.

Before You Start · Reading the Real Story

The reality: A Gallup study found that managers account for roughly 70% of the variance in team engagement, so when someone's performance drops, the manager's own conduct is often just as relevant as the employee's. Employment lawyers and HR researchers are consistent on a harder point too: a significant share of PIPs are never meant as genuine improvement tools. They're built specifically to create what attorneys call a "paper trail", documentation used to justify a termination decision that was already made.

Your job: not deciding who stays or goes. You're reading the paper trail itself, checking whether a plan has the real ingredients (clear goals, actual support, honest timing) or whether it's a shortcut dressed up as a process. The clearest warning signs, according to employment attorneys, are goals that shift partway through, timing that follows a complaint too closely, and targets no reasonable person could meet.

This lesson works like the one before it. You'll hear an account, check it against Valore Bank's PIP Policy, then draft the plan: what happens next, and how you'd put it to Renata, clearly, without protecting anyone who doesn't deserve it.

01The Policy

This is the actual language from Valore Bank's Performance Improvement Plan Policy. Read it once in full. If a section feels dense, click "Plain English" to see it broken down, with a real example.

VALORE BANK
Human Resources
Document HR-3.4
Effective January 1, 2026
Owner Human Resources Department
Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) Policy

This policy applies to all managers initiating a formal Performance Improvement Plan for a direct report. It governs when a PIP may be issued, what it must contain, how it must be run, how it concludes, and the protections in place against its misuse.

Section 1
Eligibility & Trigger Criteria

A PIP may only be initiated after the employee has already received clear, documented feedback about the specific performance gap on at least one prior occasion, at least two weeks before the plan begins. A PIP may not serve as the first formal notice of a concern, and may not be initiated within thirty days of an employee engaging in a atividade protegida por leiUma ação que a lei protege contra retaliação, como fazer uma denúncia, pedir licença, ou relatar assédio.Other ways to say this: legally protected action, whistleblowing or leave requests, unless the underlying concern was independently and thoroughly documented before that activity occurred.

Section 2
Plan Requirements & Documentation

Every PIP must be a written document specifying the specific performance gap, measurable goals tied to the employee's actual role and peer benchmarks, the resources and support the company will provide, and a fixed timeline, typically 30, 60, or 90 days. Goals may not be modified once the plan is in effect, except to make them easier to meet. A verbal instruction to "do better" does not constitute a valid PIP.

Section 3
Check-In Cadence & Manager Obligations

The employee's manager must hold and document at least one formal check-in every two weeks for the duration of the plan, at the ritmo, frequência regularA frequência regular com que algo deve acontecer, aqui usada pra exigir reuniões de acompanhamento constantes.Other ways to say this: regular rhythm, recurring schedule set out in the plan. Each check-in must address specific progress against the stated goals and must be recorded in writing and shared with the employee. A PIP in which the required check-ins were not held or documented may not be used as grounds for termination, regardless of the outcome.

Section 4
Outcomes & Decision Points

At the conclusion of the plan period, the manager must document one of three outcomes: successful completion, in which case the PIP is closed and may not be referenced in future negative performance discussions without a new, separate incident; extension, granted when partial progress was made and the specific reasons for extending are documented; or motivo/justificativa para demissãoA razão documentada e válida que justifica encerrar o vínculo empregatício de alguém.Other ways to say this: justification for dismissal, basis for firing, which requires a written summary demonstrating the employee failed to meet the specific, previously agreed goals despite the support outlined in Section 2.

Section 5
Prohibited Uses & Retaliation Protections

A PIP may never be used as a substitute for addressing a manager's own management failures, nor as a mechanism to build a record against an employee for reasons unrelated to documented performance. Any employee who believes a PIP was issued in retaliation for a protected activity, or was built with goals no reasonable person could meet, may request an revisão independenteUma análise feita por alguém sem interesse direto no caso, geralmente RH, pra garantir imparcialidade.Other ways to say this: impartial review, neutral assessment by Human Resources before the plan takes effect.

Approved by Valore Bank Human Resources Department

02The Language

Eight terms you'll need to tell a genuine improvement plan from one that's already decided the outcome.

1

A manager says an employee is "just not cutting it anymore," but can't point to a single missed deadline or metric. Without a specific, documented lacuna de desempenhoA diferença concreta e específica entre o que se espera de alguém e o que essa pessoa realmente está entregando.Other ways to say this: performance shortfall, specific deficiency, there's nothing concrete for a PIP to actually address.

Think about it
Why does the policy require a specific performance gap, not just a general impression that someone isn't performing well?
Model answer

A general impression can't be measured or improved against, and it opens the door to bias. A specific gap gives both sides something concrete to work with, and something a later review can actually check.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveSection 1 exists exactly for this: a vague impression isn't a valid trigger. Specific examples give the plan something real to work from.
RiskyProceeding on impression alone skips the documentation requirement Section 1 is built around, and leaves the plan hard to defend later.
RiskyWaiting for examples to appear on their own delays a conversation the manager should already be having directly with the employee.
2

A new PIP sets a sales target 50% higher than any rep on the team has ever hit. Asked why, the manager says, "I want them to really push." Without a fair referência, parâmetro de comparaçãoUm padrão de comparação justo, geralmente baseado no que colegas na mesma função realmente conseguem alcançar.Other ways to say this: reference point, comparison standard tied to peers, the goal isn't really achievable.

Think about it
Why does comparing a PIP's goals to peer benchmarks matter, even if the goals sound ambitious rather than obviously unfair?
Model answer

Ambition and achievability aren't the same thing. A goal no peer has ever hit isn't a stretch goal, it's a guaranteed failure dressed up as a challenge, which is exactly what Section 2 exists to catch.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveChecking the target against real peer performance is exactly what Section 2 asks for, and it's the only way to tell ambition from an unreachable bar.
RiskyCalling this ambitious skips the actual test: whether anyone in the role has ever come close to hitting it.
RiskyAdjusting the number without checking real peer data is still a guess, not a benchmark.
3

In a private conversation, a manager admits: "Honestly, I already know I want this person gone. The PIP is just so HR doesn't come after us later." The performance concerns might be real, but this reveals the plan as a pretexto, desculpaUma razão declarada que esconde o verdadeiro motivo de uma ação, geralmente pra fazer algo parecer legítimo.Other ways to say this: false justification, cover story for a decision already made.

Think about it
Why is admitting a predetermined outcome a problem, even if the employee's performance genuinely has issues?
Model answer

A pretextual PIP isn't run in good faith, so no outcome from it can be trusted, not the check-ins, not the goals, not the final decision. Real performance issues still need a real process, not a shortcut wearing its uniform.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveA stated predetermined outcome is exactly the kind of admission Legal needs to see before this plan moves any further.
RiskyReal performance issues don't excuse a process the manager has already admitted is decided in advance.
RiskyCoaching the manager to be more discreet addresses the comment, not the actual problem underneath it.
4

An employee later discovers that every minor mistake from the past year, most never even mentioned to them at the time, appeared suddenly in their PIP paperwork. It reads less like ongoing feedback and more like a rastro documentalUm conjunto de documentos criado, às vezes depois do fato, pra justificar uma decisão que já foi tomada.Other ways to say this: documentation trail, written record built after the fact being built after the fact.

Think about it
Why does building a paper trail retroactively undermine a PIP, even if every listed incident is technically true?
Model answer

The point of documentation is to give someone a real chance to correct course as issues happen, not to compile evidence after the decision is already made. True incidents assembled retroactively still fail the purpose the policy protects.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThis is the real test: contemporaneous feedback versus a record assembled after the fact. Asking the timing question exposes which one this actually is.
RiskyTruth alone doesn't validate the process. Section 1 requires the employee to have known about issues as they happened, not after.
RiskyTelling the employee to work harder responds to the symptom while ignoring that the documentation itself may be improperly built.
5

After weeks of an unreasonably harsh PIP, an employee resigns, saying the conditions made it impossible to stay. Depending on how the plan was run, this could be treated as demissão indiretaQuando a empresa não demite formalmente, mas cria condições tão ruins que a pessoa é forçada a se demitir.Other ways to say this: forced resignation, effective termination: not fired outright, but pushed out all the same.

Think about it
Why might a company be just as exposed to legal risk from a resignation as from an actual firing?
Model answer

If the conditions were designed to force someone out, the label on the exit doesn't change the underlying reality. A resignation obtained through an unreasonable process can carry the same legal weight as a termination.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThe resignation being voluntary on paper doesn't settle it. Reviewing the plan's actual conditions is what determines whether this was truly a free choice.
RiskyCalling it voluntary without reviewing the conditions ignores exactly the question that determines real legal exposure here.
RiskyManaging team perception does nothing to address whether the underlying process was actually fair.
6

A manager provides every resource promised in the plan, meets every check-in on schedule, and genuinely adjusts the approach when the employee struggles with one specific goal. Whatever the outcome, this manager is clearly running the process in boa-féAgir com intenção honesta e genuína, sem segundas intenções ou plano oculto.Other ways to say this: honest intent, genuine effort.

Think about it
Why does the manager's good faith matter separately from whether the employee ultimately succeeds or fails?
Model answer

A good-faith process protects the company even if the outcome is termination, because it shows the plan was a genuine opportunity, not a script written in advance. Good faith is about the process, not the result.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveA well-run process supports whatever honest outcome the results point to, termination included, precisely because the process itself was fair.
RiskyExtending automatically ignores what the actual results show, and turns a fair process into an indefinite one.
RiskyGood behavior during the process still needs to be checked against Section 2's actual written requirements, not assumed.
7

After a fully documented 90-day PIP with every check-in recorded, an employee still misses the agreed goals. The company moves to terminate, citing the PIP record as clear demissão por justa causaO encerramento do vínculo empregatício com base em uma razão documentada e válida, não arbitrária.Other ways to say this: justified dismissal, documented termination.

Think about it
What makes this termination different from one based only on a manager's general dissatisfaction?
Model answer

Every step, the initial gap, the goals, the check-ins, the outcome, is documented and traceable to Sections 1 through 4. That record is what separates a defensible termination from one that's just a manager's opinion with no paper trail behind it.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveWith every step properly documented, this termination stands on the record itself, not on anyone's opinion of how things went.
RiskyExtending again with no new circumstances undermines a process that was already run fully and fairly to its conclusion.
RiskySkipping the final summary throws away the one document that makes this termination defensible later.
8

An employee is placed on a PIP two weeks after filing a complaint about their manager's conduct. The manager insists the timing is a coincidence, but the pattern reads as possible retaliaçãoPunir alguém, direta ou indiretamente, por ter feito algo legalmente protegido, como uma denúncia.Other ways to say this: reprisal, punitive response.

Think about it
Why does timing alone raise a retaliation concern, even without direct proof of intent?
Model answer

Regulators and courts often treat suspicious timing as circumstantial evidence of retaliation, because intent is rarely stated outright. The burden shifts to the company to show the PIP was already justified before the complaint, not after it.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThe timing itself is enough to warrant a Section 5 independent review, before the plan takes effect, not after any damage is done.
RiskyA denial of intent doesn't resolve what the timing itself already raises as a legitimate concern.
RiskyA quiet delay doesn't address the underlying question, and can look worse later if the timing concern resurfaces.

04Your Reality

A PIP is judged less by what it says on the day it's signed, and more by whether the weeks that follow actually match it.

Discussion 01

Why might a company be just as at risk from a PIP that's technically well-written as from one that's obviously unfair?

Model answer

A well-written plan on paper says nothing about how it was actually run. If the check-ins didn't happen or the goals were quietly impossible, the document's polish doesn't protect the company at all.

Discussion 02

Why does a manager's good faith need to be judged by the process, not by whether the employee ultimately succeeds?

Model answer

Judging good faith by the outcome would mean every failed PIP looks the same as a rigged one. Judging it by the process, real support, honest check-ins, is the only way to actually tell the difference.

Discussion 03

Why is timing often the clearest signal that a PIP isn't genuine, even when nothing in the document itself looks wrong?

Model answer

Content can be written carefully to look fair. Timing is harder to disguise: a plan that follows a complaint by two weeks tells a story the document itself never will.

Performance Glossary
Performance Management
PIP
Performance Improvement Plan
A formal, written plan giving an employee a defined period and specific goals to address a documented performance gap.
"She was placed on a 90-day PIP after two quarters of missed targets."
KPI
Key Performance Indicator
A specific, measurable number used to track whether someone or something is meeting expectations.
"His main KPI this quarter is client retention, not new sales."
OKR
Objectives and Key Results
A goal-setting method pairing a broad objective with a small set of measurable results that show whether it was achieved.
"Each team sets its OKRs at the start of the quarter, then reviews them at the end."
HRBP
HR Business Partner
An HR professional assigned to work closely with a specific department or leader, rather than handling HR generally.
"Any PIP has to be reviewed by the HRBP before it's issued."
What Comes Next
Exit interview
Final departure conversation
A structured conversation held when someone leaves a company, used to gather honest feedback about their experience.
"In her exit interview, she mentioned the PIP felt rushed from day one."
Severance
Departure payment
Payment or benefits offered to an employee when their employment ends, often shaped by how and why it ended.
"The severance package included two months' pay and extended health coverage."
Notice period
Advance warning window
The amount of time required, by law or contract, between telling someone their employment is ending and it actually ending.
"His contract requires a 30-day notice period before termination takes effect."
