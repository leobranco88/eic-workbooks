The First to Know

You're not the one who decides whether something is money laundering. Compliance is. But you're often the first person to hear the sentence that should worry them, in an interview, in a hallway comment, in something an employee says without thinking twice. This lesson is about recognizing that sentence, and knowing exactly what to do with it.

Before You Start · Not Your Call to Make

The reality: You're not expected to determine whether something is money laundering. That's Compliance's job. Global AML frameworks are built on what's called the Three Lines of Defense: frontline employees, the people actually talking to clients, are the first line, trained to notice red flags and report them upward. Compliance, the second line, investigates and decides. You already sit inside that structure, whether you'd thought of it that way or not.

Your job: recognizing the sentence that matters, and moving it up fast. Industry guidance is consistent on this point: strong first-line reporting closes blind spots early, while delayed or filtered reporting is where real risk tends to sit and grow. You don't need to be certain. You need to be fast and accurate about what you heard.

This lesson works a little differently again. You'll hear an account, read the relevant policy section, then draft an actual escalation plan, concrete next steps, not just a verdict. Compliance decides what happens next. Your job is to make sure they get what they need, quickly, and that nothing you do along the way makes things worse.

Sources: "Three Lines of Defense" AML/CTF compliance framework, as described in industry guidance from sanctions.io, "The Three Lines of Defence Explained," and KYC Lookup, "3 Lines of Defence in AML: What It Means and Why It Still Matters."

01The Policy

This is the actual language from Valore Bank's AML/KYC Policy. Read it once in full. If a section feels dense, click "Plain English" to see it broken down, with a real example.

VALORE BANK
Compliance & Financial Crime
Document AML-2.1
Effective January 1, 2026
Owner Compliance Department
Anti-Money Laundering & Know Your Customer Policy

This policy applies to all Valore Bank employees who interact with clients, process transactions, or handle onboarding at any stage. It governs customer verification, enhanced scrutiny for higher-risk clients, red-flag recognition, escalation timelines, and the confidentiality obligations that follow from a report.

Section 1
Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

Before any account is opened or any new business relationship is established, employees must verify the client's identity using government-issued documentation and confirm that the origem dos recursosDe onde o dinheiro do cliente efetivamente veio, usado pra confirmar que a atividade é legítima.Other ways to say this: where the money came from, funding origin is consistent with the client's stated profile. No account may be opened, and no transaction may be processed, before this verification is complete, regardless of client seniority, urgency, or internal pressure to expedite the relationship.

Section 2
Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) for High-Risk Clients

Clients identified as pessoa politicamente expostaAlguém que ocupa ou ocupou um cargo público de destaque, ou é parente/associado próximo de quem ocupa, o que exige verificação extra.Other ways to say this: PEP, senior public official (PEPs), clients located in high-risk jurisdictions, or clients whose activity does not clearly match their stated business must undergo enhanced due diligence, including senior management approval and ongoing monitoring, before the relationship may proceed.

Section 3
Red Flags & Suspicious Activity

Employees must report any of the following to Compliance immediately upon observation: transactions fracionadoDividir um valor grande em depósitos menores de propósito, pra ficar abaixo do limite que exige notificação automática.Other ways to say this: broken into smaller amounts, split deliberately to fall just below reporting thresholds; requests to bypass or expedite standard verification steps; client reluctance to provide ownership or source-of-funds information; and any pattern of activity inconsistent with a client's known business. Employees are not required to determine whether such activity is actually illicit before reporting it.

Section 4
Escalation & Reporting Timelines

Any suspected red flag must be escalonado, encaminhado pra um nível superiorLevar um problema pra alguém com mais autoridade ou responsabilidade pra decidir, ao invés de resolver sozinho.Other ways to say this: raised to a higher level, passed upward to Compliance within one business day of observation. Compliance, not the employee who identifies the concern, is responsible for determining whether a relatório de atividade suspeitaUm documento oficial (conhecido como SAR) enviado às autoridades quando há suspeita razoável de atividade ilícita.Other ways to say this: SAR, official suspicious-activity filing (SAR) must be filed with the relevant authority. Delaying a report to gather more evidence independently is itself a policy violation.

Section 5
Confidentiality & Non-Retaliation

Employees must never inform a client, directly or indirectly, that a report has been filed or that their account is under review. This is a legal prohibition known as avisar o cliente sobre a investigaçãoAlertar, direta ou indiretamente, que uma pessoa está sob investigação. Isso é proibido por lei, mesmo que feito sem má intenção.Other ways to say this: alerting the subject, leaking the investigation, and applies even to casual or well-intentioned comments. Employees who report concerns in good faith are protected from retaliation, regardless of whether an investigation later confirms wrongdoing.

02The Language

Eight terms you'll need to recognize a red flag correctly, and to write it up the way Compliance expects.

1

During a routine review, an employee notices a client's monthly deposits jumped from $2,000 to $180,000 with no explanation on file. She mentions it to you, unsure if it's even worth bringing up. It's a clássico, de manualUsado aqui como adjetivo pra dizer que algo é um exemplo perfeito e característico de algo. Não tem relação com "livro didático".Other ways to say this: classic example of, perfect example of, model sinal de alertaUm padrão ou comportamento que indica risco e deve ser reportado, mesmo sem prova de que algo ilegal está acontecendo.Other ways to say this: warning sign, indicator: a pattern that doesn't match the client's known profile.

Think about it
Why does policy require reporting a red flag even when the employee isn't sure it means anything?
Model answer

Deciding what it means is Compliance's job, not the frontline employee's. Reporting doesn't require certainty, just noticing something inconsistent with what's expected.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThis is exactly what Section 3 asks for: report the pattern, let Compliance determine what it means. Certainty was never the requirement.
RiskyAsking the client directly, before Compliance is even aware, risks tipping off someone who may be under review before any review has started.
RiskyWaiting to confirm the pattern first is exactly the kind of delay Section 4 treats as its own violation.
2

A new corporate account lists a small local company as the owner. During the interview, the employee mentions almost in passing: "the guy who actually calls the shots is some investor in another country, he's just not on any of the paperwork." That investor may be the real beneficiário finalA pessoa que de fato controla ou lucra com uma empresa, mesmo que o nome dela não apareça nos documentos oficiais.Other ways to say this: real owner, ultimate controller, and the paperwork doesn't reflect that.

Think about it
Why does the policy care who actually controls a company, not just who's listed on paper?
Model answer

Ownership on paper can hide who really benefits from or directs an account. Verifying the true owner is what prevents someone from hiding behind a company name.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThis matches Section 1 exactly: the paperwork doesn't reflect who actually controls the account, and that gap belongs in front of Compliance.
RiskyTechnically on file doesn't mean accurate. Accepting it as valid ignores what the employee just told you directly.
RiskyFixing the paperwork quietly isn't her call, or yours. This needs Compliance's eyes before anything gets updated.
3

A client's account is registered to a company with no employees, no physical office, and no website, but it moves large sums every month. That's a classic profile of a empresa de fachadaUma empresa que existe no papel, mas tem pouca ou nenhuma operação real, às vezes legítima, às vezes usada pra esconder a origem do dinheiro.Other ways to say this: paper company, front company, sometimes entirely legitimate, but exactly the kind of structure real money laundering hides behind.

Think about it
Why isn't a shell company automatically a violation?
Model answer

Shell companies have legitimate uses, like holding companies or tax structuring, so the structure alone isn't proof of wrongdoing. What matters is whether the activity matches a plausible legitimate purpose.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThis is Section 2 working correctly: an unusual profile earns a closer look, not an automatic verdict either way.
RiskyClosing the account outright treats a legitimate structure as automatic proof of wrongdoing, which isn't how the policy, or the law, actually works.
RiskyIgnoring it misses exactly the kind of profile Section 2 asks you to escalate for a closer look.
4

An analyst mentions that one client's funds seem to move through several different accounts, in several different countries, before finally landing in a Valore Bank account. Textbook estratificação, camadasMover dinheiro através de múltiplas transações e contas, de propósito, pra dificultar rastrear a origem real dos recursos.Other ways to say this: obscuring the money trail, multi-step transfers: multiple transfers designed to obscure where the money originally came from.

Think about it
Why is layering harder to catch than a single suspicious deposit?
Model answer

Each individual transfer can look ordinary on its own. It's the combination and sequence across accounts that reveals the pattern, which is why frontline observations across different moments matter so much.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThe full sequence is the actual signal here. Compliance needs the whole pattern, not just the piece that happened to land at Valore Bank.
RiskyReporting only the final step strips out exactly the context that makes this pattern recognizable as layering in the first place.
RiskyTracing funds further back isn't the analyst's job, or yours. Section 4 asks for escalation, not independent investigation.
5

A manager tells his team, half-joking, "I don't ask clients too many questions, keeps things moving faster." An employee repeats this to you, laughing it off. It's actually a real legal risk: cegueira propositalEvitar de propósito fazer perguntas ou buscar informação, pra depois poder alegar que "não sabia" de algo.Other ways to say this: deliberate ignorance, looking the other way, deliberately avoiding facts that would otherwise require action.

Think about it
Why can avoiding questions be treated as seriously as actually knowing something was wrong?
Model answer

Regulators recognize that deliberately not asking is often a way of trying to avoid responsibility, so willful blindness can be treated legally much like actual knowledge.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveA comment like this hints at a habit across a whole team, not an isolated remark. That's worth Compliance's attention, not just yours.
RiskyTone doesn't erase content. A joking delivery doesn't make the underlying practice, if it's real, any less worth escalating.
RiskyHandling this yourself, one-on-one, skips the exact structure the Three Lines of Defense depends on: you notice, Compliance decides.
6

A young client opens an account and, within weeks, is receiving large sums and immediately forwarding them to unrelated third parties, keeping a small percentage each time. This fits the profile of a laranjaAlguém, muitas vezes sem saber, usado pra movimentar dinheiro de origem ilícita em nome de outra pessoa.Other ways to say this: unwitting intermediary, financial go-between: someone, often unknowingly, used to move illicit funds.

Think about it
Why might a money mule not realize they're doing anything wrong?
Model answer

They're sometimes recruited through fake job offers or told they're helping a friend, so their intent may be genuinely innocent even though the activity itself is a real red flag.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThe pattern itself is what triggers Section 3, not whether the client seems to understand what's happening. Intent is Compliance's question to investigate, not yours.
RiskyAssuming innocence and doing nothing lets a real pattern continue simply because the client might not realize what's happening.
RiskyConfronting the client directly risks tipping off someone who may be part of an active investigation, before Compliance even knows.
7

A branch employee mentions that a cliente sem indicação préviaUm cliente que chega sem nenhuma indicação ou relacionamento anterior com o banco, o que costuma exigir mais verificação.Other ways to say this: unreferred client, cold client, no referral, no prior relationship, no history with the bank, asked to open a large account with an unusually complex ownership structure, all in a single visit.

Think about it
Why does a walk-in client with no prior relationship deserve more scrutiny than a referred one?
Model answer

A referral usually means someone already vouches for the client's legitimacy. A walk-in with no history offers no such context, so the bank has to build that picture entirely on its own.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveThe combination matters here: no history plus real complexity is exactly the profile Section 2 asks to be reviewed more closely, not waved through or rejected outright.
RiskyTreating this like any ordinary case ignores the specific combination of no relationship and unusual structure together.
RiskyDeclining outright isn't your call, or the branch employee's, to make without Compliance ever reviewing the case.
8

An onboarding associate mentions that the system flagged a partial name match against a sanctions list during account opening, but she cleared it herself because "the spelling was a little different." That's not how triagem de sançõesO processo de checar clientes contra listas oficiais de pessoas ou empresas proibidas de fazer negócios.Other ways to say this: watchlist screening, sanctions check is supposed to work.

Think about it
Why shouldn't an employee clear a sanctions match on their own, even if it looks like an easy call?
Model answer

Sanctions screening exists specifically to catch near-matches a person might dismiss too quickly. Clearing it individually removes the second check the process is designed to have.

Your call, reporting this to Renata
Solid moveEscalating even an already-cleared match restores the second check that a sanctions hit is supposed to get, regardless of how confident the first read felt.
RiskyExperience isn't the safeguard here. The whole point of screening is a second, independent check, not individual judgment alone.
RiskyAsking her to be more careful next time still leaves this specific match self-resolved instead of properly escalated.

04Your Reality

Noticing is only half the job. What you do in the minutes right after is what actually protects everyone, the client, the employee who told you, and the bank.

Discussion 01

Why does the policy ask employees to report red flags without first deciding whether the activity is actually illegal?

Model answer

Frontline employees don't have the full picture across accounts, clients, and time that Compliance does. Asking them to judge first, instead of just reporting, would filter out exactly the reports the system depends on.

Discussion 02

Why is a fast, unremarkable-looking report often more valuable to Compliance than a slow, thoroughly investigated one?

Model answer

Speed preserves Compliance's ability to act before a pattern continues or a client moves funds elsewhere. A delay spent gathering more proof can cost the exact window that made the report useful.

Discussion 03

What makes tipping off dangerous even when the person saying it has good intentions?

Model answer

Intent doesn't change the effect: once a client knows they're being watched, they can move funds, destroy records, or simply disappear, closing the window an investigation needed to work.

AML/KYC Glossary
Core Acronyms
AML
Anti-Money Laundering
Laws, policies, and procedures designed to stop criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income.
"The bank's AML team reviews every large or unusual transaction."
KYC
Know Your Customer
The process of verifying a client's identity and understanding their business before starting a relationship.
"KYC has to be completed before any account can be opened."
CDD
Customer Due Diligence
The standard identity and source-of-funds checks required for every client, regardless of risk level.
"Basic CDD includes verifying a government-issued ID and confirming the client's stated occupation."
EDD
Enhanced Due Diligence
Additional scrutiny required for higher-risk clients, such as PEPs or clients in high-risk jurisdictions.
"The account needed EDD because the client is a politically exposed person."
PEP
Politically Exposed Person
Someone who holds, or is closely connected to someone who holds, a prominent public position, which raises their risk profile.
"A former minister's spouse would typically be treated as a PEP."
SAR
Suspicious Activity Report
The official report filed with authorities when Compliance determines a case warrants government review.
"Compliance decided the pattern was serious enough to file a SAR."
On the Ground
Red flag
Warning sign
A pattern or behavior that should be reported for review, without needing proof that something illegal happened.
"Deposits just under the reporting limit are a classic red flag."
Tipping off
Alerting the subject
Letting a client know, even indirectly, that they're under review. This is illegal, not just against policy.
"Saying 'we just need extra paperwork because of a review' can count as tipping off."
Escalate
Report upward
Passing a concern to someone with the authority to act on it, instead of resolving it on your own.
"The right move is always to escalate, not to investigate on your own."
