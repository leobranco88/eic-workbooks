PART I · TEXT IQuestions 01 to 06
Reading

The trouble with learning from mistakes

Why the brain protects us from the lesson we most need

First and foremost, we should admit something uncomfortable: most of us are terrible at learning from our mistakes. Psychologists have studied this tendency for decades, and their findings are remarkably consistent. When an experiment fails, when a project collapses, when a friendship ends badly, the human brain rarely responds by asking what went wrong. It responds by protecting itself.

Dr. Amara Osei, a sociologist who has been researching failure since 2011, calls this "burying your head in the sand." Her participants were asked to describe a recent professional mistake. Almost all of them acknowledged that the mistake had happened, but only a third could explain what they would do differently. The rest blamed circumstances, colleagues, or bad luck.

This is not a fact of life we are stuck with. Osei argues that people who are genuinely driven improve because they have built one specific habit: they write down what happened before their memory of it softens. It sounds simple. It is easier said than done. By the time most of us get around to reflecting on a failure, the story has already been rewritten in our favor.

Interestingly, the research has come full circle. Osei's newest work suggests that the same protective instinct that stops us learning also stops us from being paralyzed by shame. The mechanism that undermines our growth may be the one that keeps us functioning.

Fictional text, written by the EIC Creative Department from the Unit 1 theme of Life 6. The researcher and the figures quoted are fictional, created for assessment purposes.

What is the main purpose of Text I?

(A)To teach a memorization technique to students preparing for exams.
(B)To criticize psychologists who study human behavior without evidence.
(C)To compare the professional performance of optimists and pessimists.
(D)To explain why we rarely learn from our mistakes and what research suggests.

Resposta (D)

The text presents a problem (we protect ourselves instead of analyzing the mistake), supports it with research and closes on a nuance. It teaches no technique, attacks no researchers and compares no personality types.

02

In the second paragraph, the expression burying your head in the sand describes the behavior of someone who:

(A)admits the mistake in public and apologizes to the people involved.
(B)refuses to face something unpleasant, pretending it does not exist.
(C)studies their own mistake in detail until they understand its cause.
(D)blames themselves for everything that ends up going wrong in life.

Resposta (B)

Bury your head in the sand is avoiding an unpleasant reality, acting as though it were not there. The next sentences confirm it: most admitted the mistake but could not say what they would do differently.

03

In the third paragraph, It is easier said than done indicates that the habit described by Osei is:

(A)simple to understand, but difficult to put into practice.
(B)impossible to learn after a certain age in adulthood.
(C)effective only when it is written down by someone else.
(D)too expensive for most people to be able to adopt it.
Word focus · Unit 1
Resposta (A)

Easier said than done is the gap between saying and doing. The text reinforces it: by the time we reflect on a failure, memory has already rewritten the story in our favor.

04

The finding that only a third could explain what they would do differently suggests that most participants:

(A)could not remember any recent professional mistake at all.
(B)acknowledged the mistake but did not turn it into learning.
(C)deliberately lied to the researcher about what had happened.
(D)blamed their own lack of preparation for the whole situation.
Inference (Unit 1)
Resposta (B)

The text says almost all acknowledged the mistake, yet only a third could say what they would change. Acknowledging is not learning. The rest shifted responsibility to circumstances, colleagues or bad luck.

05

In a sociologist who has been researching failure since 2011, the verb form was chosen because it:

(A)stresses the finished result of research that ended back in 2011.
(B)describes a research habit that is repeated every year since 2011.
(C)stresses the duration of a process begun in the past and still going.
(D)describes an action happening right now and ending straight after.
Perfect forms · Unit 2a
Resposta (C)

Have/has been + -ing with since is Family P2: duration and process, tying the past to now. If the focus were the result (Family P1), it would be has researched. The research started in 2011, it did not end there.

06

In Her participants were asked to describe a recent professional mistake, the agent is left out because:

(A)it is obvious from context and naming it would add nothing.
(B)the sentence is in the past continuous, which takes no agent.
(C)the verb ask never accepts the structure with by at all.
(D)the agent is unknown, which leaves the sentence incomplete.
Passive forms · Unit 2b
Resposta (A)

Family PA: the agent is omitted when it is obvious, unknown or irrelevant. Here it is obvious, since Osei ran the study. The sentence is past simple passive, were + asked, and leaving out the agent breaks nothing.

PART II · TEXT IIQuestions 07 to 12
Reading

The last of the Ama

Two thousand years of diving, and one generation left

For more than two thousand years, the Ama divers of Japan have been slipping into the cold water off the Pacific coast without oxygen tanks, holding their breath for up to two minutes at a time. They come back up with abalone, seaweed and, in earlier centuries, pearls. Almost all of them are women.

Today fewer than two thousand Ama are still working, and the average age is close to seventy. Kimiko Nakamura started diving at nineteen. "It took me a whole season to find my feet," she says. "After that, it became second nature."

The work has never been safe. Ama learn to read the current, the temperature and their own bodies, and they are taught never to cut corners. A diver who ignores the signs and stays down for a few extra seconds may not come back up. "We always err on the side of caution," Nakamura says. "You can replace an abalone. You cannot replace a person."

What is disappearing is more than a livelihood. It is a body of knowledge that was never written down. By 2040, researchers estimate, the tradition will have vanished from most of the coastline. Nakamura's daughter did not follow in her mother's footsteps; she moved to Osaka at twenty-two and works in logistics.

"She got out of it," Nakamura says, and then laughs. "I do not blame her. This job is not all fun and games."

Text written by the EIC Creative Department from the Unit 2 theme of Life 6. The Ama and the practice of free diving are real; the diver quoted is a fictional character.
07

Text II is organized mainly around the idea that the Ama tradition:

(A)has started growing again as young Japanese women take it up.
(B)was always a male activity that only recently accepted women.
(C)was banned by the Japanese government for safety reasons at sea.
(D)is disappearing, taking with it knowledge never written down.
Critical thinking · purpose (Unit 2)
Resposta (D)

The text moves from the shrinking number of divers and the high average age to the 2040 projection, and closes with the daughter who did not follow her mother. The point is the loss of an unrecorded body of knowledge.

08

In It took me a whole season to find my feet, Nakamura means that it took a whole season to:

(A)learn how to swim in open water without any help at all.
(B)get used to the new situation and feel comfortable in it.
(C)manage to buy her own diving equipment and wetsuit set.
(D)be accepted by the older divers already in her own group.
Word focus foot/feet · Unit 2
Resposta (B)

Find your feet is settling into a new situation until it feels natural. The next line confirms it: it became second nature.

09

In the third paragraph, they are taught never to cut corners means that the Ama learn never to:

(A)dive alone in the open sea, far away from the coastline.
(B)disagree with the older divers working in their own group.
(C)skip steps in the procedure in order to save a little time.
(D)carry more equipment with them than would really be needed.
Idioms: safety · Unit 2
Resposta (C)

Cut corners is doing something faster by leaving parts out, risking poor work. The context is safety, and the next quotation reinforces it: we always err on the side of caution.

10

The line You can replace an abalone. You cannot replace a person. works in the text as:

(A)a technical note on the current market value of abalone in Japan.
(B)a piece of dramatic language that uses sharp contrast for emphasis.
(C)a criticism of the heavy consumption of seafood on the Japanese coast.
(D)an explanation of why free diving is banned in certain coastal areas.
Critical thinking · dramatic language (Unit 2)
Resposta (B)

Two short sentences, same structure, opposite meaning. That is deliberate contrast, one of the markers of dramatic language studied in the unit: the effect comes from the form, not from the fact.

11

In By 2040, the tradition will have vanished from most of the coastline, the verb form expresses:

(A)an action in progress at the moment when the text was written.
(B)an action that began in the past and continues to this day.
(C)a prediction with no set deadline, the same as will vanish.
(D)an action that will be complete before a point in the future.
Perfect forms · Unit 2a
Resposta (D)

Future perfect: the reference point is 2040 and the action will be finished before it. Family P1, focus on the completed fact. Will vanish would only say it happens at some point, not that it happens before 2040.

12

Which of the following extracts from Text II is in the passive voice?

(A)Ama learn to read the current, the temperature and their own bodies.
(B)A diver who ignores the signs and stays down for a few extra seconds may not come back up.
(C)It is a body of knowledge that was never written down.
(D)Nakamura's daughter did not follow in her mother's footsteps.
Passive forms · Unit 2b
Resposta (C)

Was written is be + past participle, past simple passive with the agent omitted (Family PA). The other three are active: learn, may not come back up and did not follow.

13

Complete the sentence: "The storm was terrifying, but by midnight everyone was home ."

(A)safe and sound
(B)short and sweet
(C)bits and pieces
(D)fun and games
Binomial pairs · Unit 1
Resposta (A)

Safe and sound means unharmed, especially after danger. Short and sweet means briefly and pleasantly, bits and pieces are odds and ends, and fun and games is amusement.

14

Complete the sentence: "People travel from to see the ceremony, some of them from other continents."

(A)to and fro
(B)as and when
(C)far and wide
(D)first and foremost
Binomial pairs · Unit 1
Resposta (C)

Far and wide stresses that people come from a very large area. To and fro is back and forth, as and when is whenever needed, and first and foremost means above all.

15

Complete the sentence: "You have just graduated, you speak three languages and you are only twenty-two. ."

(A)The world is your oyster
(B)Love is blind
(C)The story of my life
(D)Neither here nor there
Word focus: life · Unit 1
Resposta (A)

The world is your oyster says someone can do whatever they want, with everything in their favor. The other three do not match the tone of opportunity in the sentence.

16

Complete the sentence: "The volunteers came from : doctors, students, retired teachers, taxi drivers."

(A)all walks of life
(B)the life of the party
(C)a fact of life
(D)far and wide
Word focus: life · Unit 1
Resposta (A)

All walks of life describes people from very different backgrounds and occupations, which is exactly what the list shows. Far and wide would be about geographical distance, not variety of background.

17

"Theo hates leaving anything to chance. He wants to know exactly what everyone is doing, and he redoes other people's work if it is not exactly how he pictured it." Theo is:

(A)a free spirit
(B)a joker
(C)a control freak
(D)a dreamer
Personality and identity · Unit 1
Resposta (C)

A control freak cannot stand leaving things to chance and needs to be in control at all times. A free spirit is the opposite, a joker is the comedian and a dreamer is the impractical one.

18

"She had no map, no signal and no guide, so she used the position of the sun and got the group back to the road before dark." The adjective that best describes this person is:

(A)resourceful
(B)privileged
(C)conscientious
(D)cerebral

Resposta (A)

Resourceful is solving problems cleverly with little help, which is exactly what she did. Conscientious is careful in one's work, privileged is advantaged and cerebral is intellectual.

19

"If you keep protecting him from every small difficulty, he will never learn to deal with anything on his own." The extract describes someone who is:

(A)cutting corners
(B)doing things by the book
(C)getting off on the wrong foot
(D)wrapping him in cotton wool

Resposta (D)

Wrap someone in cotton wool is overprotecting far beyond what is reasonable. Cut corners is the opposite, and do things by the book is following rules exactly, without the idea of excessive protection.

20

Complete the sentence: "I have been meaning to answer that email for three weeks, but I still have not it."

(A)got over
(B)got around to
(C)got away with
(D)got by

Resposta (B)

Get around to is finally doing something you have been putting off, which fits for three weeks. Get over is to recover from, get away with is to escape punishment and get by is to manage.

21

"At the party, Theo asked my colleague when the baby was due. She is not pregnant." In this situation, Theo:

(A)got his foot in the door
(B)had cold feet
(C)found his feet
(D)put his foot in it

Resposta (D)

Put your foot in it is saying something without thinking and embarrassing someone. Have cold feet is hesitating before a big decision, and get your foot in the door is gaining a first opening in a career.

PART IV · LANGUAGE STRUCTUREQuestions 22 to 30
22

Complete the sentence: "Ten years ago, Marina in a small fishing village every summer."

(A)stays
(B)is staying
(C)stayed
(D)has stayed
Time adverbials · Unit 1a
Resposta (C)

A classic trap: every summer looks like it calls for the present, but the trigger in charge is ten years ago, which fixes the sentence in the past. Repeated past action takes past simple, not continuous.

23

Complete the sentence: "More and more teenagers volunteer work these days."

(A)are doing
(B)do
(C)did
(D)have done
Time adverbials · Unit 1a
Resposta (A)

More and more and these days signal a changing trend, not a fixed habit. Trends belong to Family Continuous, even when the sentence looks like a general statement.

24

Complete the sentence: "That wetsuit to my sister, not to me."

(A)is belonging
(B)is being belonged
(C)has been belonging
(D)belongs
Continuous aspect · Unit 1b
Resposta (D)

Belong is a state verb: it describes how something already is, with no marked beginning or end, so it does not take the continuous. It does not go into the passive either.

25

Complete the sentence: "My brother my charger without asking. It drives me crazy."

(A)always takes
(B)is always taking
(C)always is taking
(D)has always taken
Continuous aspect · Unit 1b
Resposta (B)

A repeated complaint with always must go into the continuous in English, and always sits between be and the verb: is always taking. The second sentence confirms the tone of irritation.

26

Complete the sentence: "Kimiko every morning at five, but this week she later because of the storm."

(A)is diving / dives
(B)dives / dive
(C)dives / is diving
(D)is diving / is diving
Continuous aspect · Unit 1b
Resposta (C)

A fixed routine (every morning at five) is Family Simple: dives. A temporary exception (this week) is Family Continuous: is diving. Both families live in the same sentence.

27

Complete the sentence: "By the time the rescue team reached the ledge, the climber there for six hours."

(A)has been waiting
(B)had been waiting
(C)was waiting
(D)had waited
Perfect forms · Unit 2a
Resposta (B)

The reference point is in the past (by the time the team reached) and for six hours marks duration. Past perfect continuous, Family P2. Has been waiting would anchor it to now, which the sentence does not do.

28

Complete the sentence: "Next March, Kimiko for fifty years."

(A)will dive
(B)will be diving
(C)will have been dived
(D)will have been diving
Perfect forms · Unit 2a
Resposta (D)

A reference point in the future (next March) plus duration (for fifty years) calls for future perfect continuous. Will be diving would only describe what she is doing that day, without the fifty years behind it.

29

Complete the sentence: "The safety rules three times since the accident in 2019."

(A)have revised
(B)has been revised
(C)have been revised
(D)have being revised
Passive forms · Unit 2b
Resposta (C)

The passive inside the present perfect: have been + past participle. The subject rules is plural, so it takes have, not has. Without been the sentence turns active, making the rules the ones doing the revising.

30

Which sentence allows the agent with by to be left out without loss of meaning?

(A)The report was reviewed by Ms. Silva, our head of safety.
(B)The portrait was painted by Frida Kahlo.
(C)The new rule was written by the students themselves.
(D)My wallet was stolen by someone at the station.
Passive forms · Unit 2b
Resposta (D)

By someone adds nothing: the agent is unknown and irrelevant, the typical Family PA case. In the other three, knowing who did it changes the meaning or is the whole point of the sentence (Family PB).
