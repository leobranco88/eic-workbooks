Discovery call

You know how to interview people. Now you will interview a buyer. Ask first, pitch later.

A discovery call is the first call where a sales rep learns what the buyer needs. You will read two short texts, practice the questions of a first call, and see the call through the eyes of the buyer. Tap or hover over any highlighted phrase to see it in Portuguese.

Two texts, two angles

Read each one on its own page. Below is a short summary to help you.

What to ask

Avoid the land of no decision: 6 critical questions for every discovery call

Bardia Shahali · Intercom · 2018

Shahali says reps often spend hours on demos and decks, but assume they can wing it on a discovery call. He argues the call deserves the same preparation, and shares six questions he asks every time. They cover what made the buyer look, the problem, how the buyer copes today, how success is measured, how far along the buyer is, and who else is involved. His goal is to keep deals out of the land of no decision.

“A two-way conversation.”

Look for the phrase softball question in the text. Which of the six is it, and why is it a good way to start?

How not to start

The Popular But Totally Flawed Way Reps Are Beginning Discovery Calls

Aja Frost · HubSpot

Frost says many reps open by asking the buyer what they already know about the company’s products. It seems efficient, but it moves the conversation to the seller, and she says it hurts the rep’s credibility. Her advice: open with questions about the buyer’s goals and responsibilities, and ask about product knowledge later. She also describes the buyer’s journey: the more the buyer already knows, the shorter your explanation can be.

“Damages the rep’s credibility.”

The six questions
  1. What made you start looking at tools like ours?
  2. What are you trying to solve?
  3. How do you handle it today?
  4. Which numbers do you use to track your goals?
  5. How far along are you? What is your timeline, and who else are you looking at?
  6. Who else will take part in the decision?

4

The call

Five steps for your first discovery call.

  1. 1Set the agenda

    “Thanks for your time. I’d like to spend a few minutes on your goals, then answer your questions.”

    Say what the call is for. A short agenda respects their time.

    In a real call
    Buyer
    Sounds good. I have thirty minutes.
    You
    Perfect. Let’s start with you.
  2. 2Start with their goals

    “What are your main goals this quarter?”

    Ask about them, not about your product. This is Frost’s advice.

    In a real call
    Buyer
    We want to make onboarding faster for new customers.
    You
    Faster in what way? Tell me more.
  3. 3Find the problem

    “What are you trying to solve, and how do you handle it today?”

    Two of Shahali’s six questions in one sentence. Listen for the pain point.

    In a real call
    Buyer
    We do it by hand in spreadsheets, and it takes too long.
    You
    How much time does that take each week?
  4. 4Ask how they measure it

    “Which numbers do you use to track this?”

    The numbers tell you what a good result looks like for them.

    In a real call
    Buyer
    Mostly how many days a new customer needs to get started.
    You
    And where are you today compared with your goal?
  5. 5Agree on the next step

    “Who else should join our next call? Would Thursday work?”

    Never end a call without a date. This keeps the deal out of the land of no decision.

    In a real call
    Buyer
    Thursday works. I’ll bring our finance lead.
    You
    Great. I’ll send an invite and a short summary today.

Too blunt → Say this

Say the better version out loud first. Then tap the row to check.

What do you know about us?“What are your main goals this quarter?”
Why haven’t you fixed this yet?“What has made this hard to solve so far?”

Let me tell you about our product.“Before I explain anything, could you tell me what you’re working on?”
Are you going to buy?“What would you need to see to feel ready to decide?”

The answer

The call is going well, then the buyer says something that could end it. Here is what you can say next.

Buyer

“Just send me the pricing.”

You

“Happy to. Two quick questions first, so I send the right one.”

Buyer

“We’re just looking around.”

You

“That’s fine. What made you start looking?”

Buyer

“I need to check with my team.”

You

“Of course. Who should I include in the next call?”

Your turn

The call has just started. The buyer says: “I only have fifteen minutes.” Reply in two or three sentences. Use steps 1 and 2.

“Of course, I’ll keep it short. I’d like to spend the first few minutes on your goals. Can you tell me about your role and what you’re working on right now?”

5

The other side of the call

The buyer is not waiting for a pitch. Here is where they are, the words they use, and what you already know.

Where is the buyer? Ask first, then adjust.

Frost describes three stages of the buyer’s journey. A buyer who knows very little needs more explanation. One who is close to a decision needs less. You can also invite the buyer to stop you if you cover something they already know.

6

Watch out

Four Portuguese traps on a sales call.

I want to know your necessities.I want to understand your needs.necessidades = needs (necessities are basic things, like food)
Actually, we use another tool.Currently, we use another tool.atualmente = currently (actually = in fact)
Can we combine a time?Can we agree on a time?combinar = to agree on, to arrange
Who is the responsible for this?Who is in charge of this?o responsável = the person in charge
