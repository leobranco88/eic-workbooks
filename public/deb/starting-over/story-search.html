Two Scenarios

You each get one half of Marina's story. Ask your partner about the half you don't have, questions ending in a preposition, and write down what they tell you.

You have Scenario 1: The New Job. Scenario 2 is your partner's, ask them about it.

This might be your own scenario, or your partner's. Tap to reveal if you get stuck.

On her first day, Marina's manager, Jonah, introduced her to the whole design team over coffee. She was nervous about making a good first impression, but everyone seemed genuinely interested in her background. Her desk was next to Priya, a senior designer who has been working at the studio for six years, and Priya immediately insisted on showing her around the building.

By Wednesday, Marina was already responsible for a small part of the mobile app redesign, though she was still getting used to the software the team used, which was different from what she knew in Brazil. On Thursday afternoon, the studio held a small welcome lunch, and her coworkers asked her dozens of questions, mostly about food and the weather back home.

Marina admitted she was still afraid of speaking up in big meetings, worried that her accent would distract people from what she was saying. Jonah reassured her: the team cared more about good ideas than a perfect accent. By Friday, she felt something she hadn't expected: she was actually looking forward to Monday. For the first time since she arrived, she wasn't just surviving the job, she was starting to enjoy it.

Marina's apartment was on the fourth floor of an old building near a park, and her landlord, Mr. Chen, warned her on day one that the heating system was unpredictable. She spent her first weekend assembling furniture and was surprised by how much she depended on YouTube tutorials to get it right.

Her next-door neighbor, Doris, an elderly woman, knocked on her door the second night with a plate of cookies, insisting on welcoming every new tenant personally. Marina was thankful for the gesture but was still adjusting to the silence of the building at night, so different from the noise of her old street in São Paulo.

During her second week, she discovered a small problem: the kitchen sink leaked whenever she used the dishwasher, and Mr. Chen took almost five days to send someone to fix it. She complained about the delay to Doris, who laughed and said it was completely normal for the building.

Slowly, Marina started relying on a nearby café for her morning coffee, and the barista, a young man named Felix, began remembering her order by the second week. She wasn't dreaming of home quite as often anymore.
