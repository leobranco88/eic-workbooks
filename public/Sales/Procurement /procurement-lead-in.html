Two sides of every deal

The buyer and the supplier

Every procurement process involves at least two sides. One side needs something. The other side has it. The deal only happens if both sides communicate clearly — and in international trade, that language is English.

Althea Industrial

The Supplier

Why English matters here

The language is part of the deal

A poorly written RFQ invites confusion. An unclear Purchase Order creates disputes. A missing amendment becomes a legal problem. And when something goes wrong, the first thing both sides read is the contract — written in English, interpreted by lawyers who read English.

What goes wrong
When language fails

Ambiguous delivery terms. Missing PO numbers on invoices. Unclear payment terms. A force majeure clause nobody read until it mattered. These are real problems that cost real money.

What goes right
When language works

A clear RFQ gets better quotations. A precise PO prevents disputes. A well-written email at the right moment saves a relationship. And a buyer who reads a contract carefully notices the clause that protects their company.

Warm-up

Talk about it before you start

01

What is the most difficult part of buying something from an international supplier — the price, the lead time, or the communication?

Example answer "For me, the hardest part is usually the lead time. The price we can negotiate, but if the supplier is in Asia and we need something in two weeks, there is very little we can do."
02

Have you ever received a quotation that was missing important information? What was missing and what did you do?

Example answer "Yes — a supplier sent us a quotation with no lead time and no payment terms. We had to go back and ask for everything again. It cost us four days and a lot of emails."
03

In your experience, what happens when a supplier misses a delivery deadline? Who is responsible, and what is the process?

Example answer "In our company, we first check the contract to see if there is a penalty clause. Then we contact the supplier formally, in writing, and open a claim. Everything goes through email so we have a record."
04

What makes you trust a supplier you have never worked with before? What do you look for in their first response?

Example answer "I look at how they respond to the RFQ. If they answer quickly, provide all the information, and use clear language — that already tells me a lot about how they operate."
05

Have you ever had to negotiate a payment term or a delivery condition in English? What was the hardest part?

Example answer "The hardest part for me was the tone. I knew what I wanted to say — I wanted to push back on their payment terms — but I was not sure how to do it in English without sounding rude."
06

What is the difference between a Purchase Order and a purchase agreement? Have you worked with both?

What the research says A Purchase Order is a single transaction commitment — it covers one order and expires on delivery. A purchase agreement governs a longer-term relationship and includes rules for all future transactions. Research shows that companies with formal supplier agreements experience significantly fewer disputes: a 2021 Deloitte Procurement Survey found that structured supplier contracts reduced claims by an average of 34% compared to PO-only arrangements.
07

When a problem happens after the goods arrive — damaged packaging, wrong quantity, missing items — what is the first step in your company?

Example answer "The first step is to document everything before touching the goods — photos, a written record of what arrived, and a note of the date and time. Then we contact the supplier formally. Acting fast matters because some contracts have a short window for claims."
08

Think about the last time you had to write a formal email to a supplier in English. What was difficult — vocabulary, tone, or something else?

Example answer "For me, it was always the opening. I never knew how to start a formal complaint email in English without sounding either too aggressive or too informal. In Portuguese it comes naturally — in English I had to think about every word."
09

Is procurement in your company mostly reactive — solving problems as they happen — or strategic — building supplier relationships over time?

What the research says Most procurement functions in Brazil operate reactively, according to a 2022 ILOS survey of industrial buyers: 68% of respondents described their purchasing as "demand-driven" rather than "supplier development-oriented." Companies that shifted to strategic procurement — with approved supplier lists, performance reviews, and long-term agreements — reported 22% lower total acquisition costs over a three-year period.
10

What is one thing a supplier could do that would immediately make you want to work with them again?

Example answer "Be proactive about problems. If a delivery is going to be late, I want to hear it from them before I have to ask. A supplier who tells me bad news early is one I can plan around. A supplier who goes silent is one I will replace."

Vocabulary

penalty clause
penalidade contratual
A financial consequence applied when one party fails to meet the contract terms — such as late delivery or quality failure.
Documents & Process
payment terms
condições de pagamento
The agreed rules about when and how a buyer will pay the seller — for example, 30 days after the invoice date.
Documents & Process
force majeure
força maior
A contract provision that frees both parties from obligations when extraordinary events — such as disasters or conflicts — make fulfilment impossible.
Logistics & Operations
acknowledge
confirmar o recebimento / acusar o recibo
To formally confirm that a document was received and understood. 'Please acknowledge receipt of this PO.'
Grammar & Usage
supposed to
era para / devia
Fixed expression for plans, agreements, or expectations. Not for personal opinions.
""The delivery was supposed to arrive on Friday.""
Legal & Contracts
Non-conforming goods
Produtos Não Conformes
Goods that do not meet the specifications, quality standards, or requirements defined in the contract or purchase order. The buyer has the right to reject non-conforming goods and request replacement or a price reduction.
Grammar & Usage
must
deve / é obrigado a
Strong obligation — a rule, legal requirement, or non-negotiable condition.
""All invoices must include the PO number.""
Logistics & Operations
stockout
ruptura de estoque
When stock reaches zero and production or sales cannot continue. Every day of stockout has a direct financial cost.
Documents & Process
quotation
cotação / orçamento formal
A formal document from a supplier stating the price, quantity, and conditions under which they will sell their goods.
Logistics & Operations
resubmit
reenviar / submeter novamente
To send a corrected document again after an error was found. 'Please resubmit the invoice with the correct unit price.'
Legal & Contracts
Scope of supply
Escopo de Fornecimento
The precise definition of what the supplier is responsible for delivering — including goods, services, documentation, packaging, and installation if applicable. A well-defined scope prevents disputes about what was and was not included in the contract price.
Documents & Process
Incoterm
incoterm
Internationally recognised trade terms that define where responsibility transfers from seller to buyer, and who pays for shipping and insurance.
Logistics & Operations
vessel
navio / embarcação
A ship used for transporting cargo. 'We have confirmed vessel booking' = space on the ship is reserved.
Legal & Contracts
Arbitration
Arbitragem
A private dispute resolution process where a neutral third party (arbitrator) makes a binding decision instead of a court. Common in international contracts because it avoids the complexity of litigating across different legal systems. The ICC (International Chamber of Commerce) is a leading arbitration institution.
Documents & Process
clause
cláusula
A specific condition or requirement written into a contract or agreement.
Logistics & Operations
reorder
reabastecer / fazer novo pedido
To place a new purchase order for a product that is running low. 'We need to reorder HX-44 immediately.'
Quality & Compliance
compatibility
compatibilidade
Whether a component will work correctly with the buyer's existing equipment or system.
Quality & Compliance
inspection
inspeção
A physical check of goods — either at the factory before shipping (outgoing) or at the warehouse on arrival (incoming).
Logistics & Operations
invoice
fatura
A document from the supplier requesting payment for goods delivered. Must match the PO and GRN to be processed.
Legal & Contracts
Request for Quotation (RFQ)
Pedido de Cotação
A formal document sent by a buyer to one or more suppliers asking them to provide a price and delivery conditions for specific goods or services. It is not an order — it is a request for information.
Legal & Contracts
Confidentiality clause
Cláusula de Confidencialidade
A contract clause that prohibits one or both parties from disclosing sensitive information — such as pricing, technical specifications, or business plans — to third parties. Also known as an NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) when it is a standalone document.
Legal & Contracts
Liability cap
Limite de Responsabilidade
A contractual limit on the maximum financial compensation one party can claim from the other. Suppliers often propose low liability caps to protect themselves from large claims. Buyers should always check if the cap covers the full value of the order — and negotiate upward if necessary.
Logistics & Operations
freight
frete / carga
The goods being transported, or the cost of transporting them. 'Freight costs' = the price of shipping.
Grammar & Usage
should
deveria / acho que
Use for advice or personal opinion. Not for agreements or contracts.
""I think we should ask for better payment terms.""
Logistics & Operations
warehouse
armazém / depósito
The physical location where goods are stored after arrival and before being used in production or distributed.
Legal & Contracts
Net 30 / Net 60
Pagamento em 30 / 60 dias
Standard payment terms meaning the buyer must pay within 30 (or 60) days of the invoice date. Net 60 is common in B2B procurement and gives the buyer more time to manage cash flow. Suppliers may offer early payment discounts (e.g. 2/10 Net 30 = 2% discount if paid within 10 days).
Legal & Contracts
Governing law
Lei Aplicável / Foro
The jurisdiction whose laws will be used to interpret the contract and resolve disputes. In international procurement, this is critical — if a dispute goes to court orarbitrationA private dispute resolution process — a neutral arbitrator decides instead of a court. / Processo privado de resolução de disputas — um árbitro neutro decide em vez de um tribunal., the governing law determines the outcome. Always check with legal before accepting a foreign governing law.
Grammar & Usage
certificate of conformance
certificado de conformidade
A document from the supplier confirming goods meet the agreed specifications.
""Every batch is supposed to come with a certificate of conformance.""
Quality & Compliance
tolerance
tolerância
The acceptable range of variation in a measurement. E.g. ±0.05mm means the part can be 0.05mm bigger or smaller than the target.
Logistics & Operations
batch
lote
A group of products made in the same production run. Each batch has a unique reference for traceability — critical in chemicals and regulated industries.
Documents & Process
procurement
compras / suprimentos
The full process of finding, evaluating, and buying goods or services for a business.
Documents & Process
lead time
prazo de produção e entrega
The total time between placing an order and receiving the goods.
Logistics & Operations
specification
especificação técnica
The exact technical requirements for a product — dimensions, materials, purity, tolerances. The supplier must meet every point.
Documents & Process
MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity)
quantidade mínima de pedido
The smallest quantity a supplier is willing to sell in a single order.
Documents & Process
Purchase Order (PO)
ordem de compra
A formal document issued by a buyer to a supplier, confirming the purchase of specific goods at an agreed price and quantity.
Documents & Process
purchase agreement
contrato de fornecimento
A formal legal document that defines the terms of an ongoing supply relationship between a buyer and a supplier.
Legal & Contracts

Warranty
Garantia
A supplier's formal commitment that goods will be free from defects for a defined period. The warranty period, start date (from delivery, installation, or first use), and what it covers are all negotiable and must be clearly specified in the contract.
Legal & Contracts
In breach of contract

When a party fails to fulfil one or more of its contractual obligations. A breach can be minor (partial non-performance) or material (serious failure that entitles the other party to terminate and claim damages). Late delivery, non-conforming goods, and failure to pay are common examples.
Documents & Process

A document sent to suppliers asking them to provide a price and delivery details for specific goods or services.
Documents & Process

A formal written change made to an existing contract or purchase order after it has been signed.
Grammar & Usage
