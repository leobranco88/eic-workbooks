Was It
Supposed To?

A supplier is supposed to deliver on time. A contract is supposed to protect both sides. Sometimes it does, sometimes it doesn't. The grammar doesn't tell you which — the situation does.

The Rule

Your opinion, or the plan?

Should = your opinion. Be supposed to = the plan or agreement. Must = obligation, no exceptions. And "supposed to" changes meaning depending on the tense — present or past.

Your opinion
should

Use it to give advice or say what you personally think is right. Nobody agreed to this — it's just your view.

"I think the supplier should offer better payment terms." (my opinion, not a rule)
The plan, the agreement
be supposed to

Use it for what was arranged, expected, or written into a contract — not your personal opinion.

"The Purchase Order is supposed to include the delivery date." (that's what the PO says)
Obligation — no choice
must

Use it for a strong requirement — a rule, a legal obligation, or something non-negotiable. There is no flexibility.

"All invoices must include the PO number." (company rule — no exceptions)
"The supplier must deliver within 30 days." (legal obligation in the contract)
The trap for Portuguese speakers

In Portuguese you say "era para o fornecedor entregar". The instinct is to translate this as "it was to deliver" — but that is wrong. The correct form is "the supplier was supposed to deliver." "Supposed to" is one fixed expression. It does not break apart the way "era para" does in Portuguese.

Supposed to: present vs. past

Present — is / are supposed to
Active expectation

The plan or rule is still in place. The situation is ongoing or has not happened yet.

"The invoice is supposed to include the PO number." (essa é a regra — sempre)
"Hua Li is supposed to deliver by Friday." (o plano ainda está ativo)
Past — was / were supposed to
Two possible outcomes

The plan existed in the past. What happened? The grammar does not tell you. Only the context does.

"Kevin was supposed to send the quotation — but he didn't." (o plano não aconteceu)
"Hua Li was supposed to deliver on time — and they did." (o plano aconteceu)

The key question for the past: did the plan work out? Look for clues in the sentence — "and they did", "but he didn't", "but it got postponed". If there is no clue, context from the situation will tell you. The grammar itself stays neutral. / A pergunta chave para o passado: o plano aconteceu? Procure pistas na frase — "and they did", "but he didn't", "but it got postponed". Se não houver pista, o contexto da situação dirá. A gramática em si é neutra.

Glossary
Key terms
supposed to
era para / devia
Fixed expression for plans, agreements, or expectations — not personal opinions.
"The delivery was supposed to arrive on Friday." (the plan said so)
should
deveria / acho que
Use for advice or personal opinion — not for agreements or contracts.
"I think we should ask for better payment terms." (my opinion)
Purchase Order (PO)
Ordem de Compra
A legally binding document that locks in price, quantity, delivery date, and payment terms.
"The PO is supposed to include the delivery date."
penalty clause
cláusula de penalidade
A contract clause specifying financial compensation for non-performance, such as late delivery.
"The penalty clause was supposed to apply from day one."
certificate of conformance
certificado de conformidade
A document from the supplier confirming that goods meet the agreed specifications.
"Every batch is supposed to come with a certificate of conformance."
Bill of Lading (B/L)
Conhecimento de Embarque
A legal shipping document required for customs clearance at the destination port.
"Hua Li was supposed to send the B/L within 24 hours of shipment."
RFQ
Pedido de Cotação
Request for Quotation — a document asking suppliers for price and delivery details.
"Suppliers are supposed to respond to an RFQ within 48 hours."
quotation
cotação
A formal offer from a supplier — not yet a commitment.
"Kevin was supposed to send the revised quotation by Monday."
liability cap
limite de responsabilidade
A contractual limit on the maximum financial compensation one party can claim.
"I think Carolina should negotiate the liability cap before signing."

Grammar Reference
Forming Questions
Should
Structure
Should + subject + verb?
"Should we ask for a revised quotation?" — Devemos pedir uma nova cotação?
"Should the legal team review this?" — O time jurídico deveria revisar isso?
"What should we do about the delay?" — O que devemos fazer sobre o atraso?
Be Supposed To
Structure
Is/Are/Was/Were + subject + supposed to + verb?
"Is the invoice supposed to include the PO number?" — A nota fiscal deve incluir o número do PO?
"Was Hua Li supposed to deliver last week?" — A Hua Li deveria ter entregado na semana passada?
"What are they supposed to send with each batch?" — O que eles devem enviar com cada lote?
Must
Structure
Must + subject + verb? → rare in questions
In practice, "must" questions are rare. We usually use "Do I have to" or "Is it required that" instead. / Na prática, perguntas com "must" são raras. Usamos "Do I have to" ou "Is it required that".
"Do I have to sign this today?" — Tenho que assinar isso hoje?
"Is it required to include both signatures?" — É obrigatório incluir as duas assinaturas?
Grammar Reference
Negative Forms
Should
Structure
Subject + should not (shouldn't) + verb
"We shouldn't accept a quotation without a lead time." — Não devemos aceitar uma cotação sem prazo.
"The buyer shouldn't sign before the legal team reviews it." — O comprador não deveria assinar antes da revisão jurídica.
Be Supposed To
Structure
Subject + is/are/was/were + not supposed to + verb
"Suppliers are not supposed to change the price after the PO is issued." — Os fornecedores não devem alterar o preço após o PO.
"The delivery was not supposed to arrive before Monday." — A entrega não deveria chegar antes de segunda.
"This clause is not supposed to apply to force majeure events." — Esta cláusula não deve se aplicar a casos de força maior.
Must
Structure + Warning
Subject + must not (mustn't) + verb
⚠️ "Must not" = prohibition, not just absence of obligation. Very strong. / "Must not" = proibição, não apenas ausência de obrigação. Muito forte.
"The supplier must not subcontract without written approval." — O fornecedor não pode subcontratar sem aprovação escrita.
"Confidential information must not be shared with third parties." — Informações confidenciais não devem ser compartilhadas.
"Don't have to" ≠ "must not"
"You don't have to sign today" = no obligation, your choice. / sem obrigação, sua escolha.
"You must not sign today" = it is forbidden. / é proibido.
