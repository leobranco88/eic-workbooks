Little Words, Big Difference — the prepositions that run customer service

"Get up," "get out," "get over," "get through," "get back" — same verb, five completely different meanings. Across every lesson so far, Renata has used tiny words like these to say exactly what she means. This is where we slow down and look at them directly.

Renata Lima · Customer Success, Altiva Biosolutions
01One Verb, Five Meanings

O verbo é sempre "get". O que muda tudo é a palavrinha depois dele.

get up
to rise, to start moving
"She gets up early."
get out
to leave, or to be sent/dispatched
"Get the shipment out today."
get over
to recover from something
"He got over the bad review."
get through
to survive or finish something hard
"We got through a tough call."
get back (to)
to return, to respond
"I'll get back to you."
The pattern: these small words — called particles — aren't decoration. Each one carries its own feeling (outward, upward, backward, through) and attaches that feeling to whatever verb it follows. Learn the particle's feeling, and you can guess dozens of phrasal verbs you've never seen before.
02The Core Particles

Sete partículas cobrem a maior parte do que a Renata (e você) precisa no dia a dia.

ParticleCore FeelingPhrasal Verbs You Already Know
OUT outward, away, finished get out a shipment, send out a reminder, sort out a problem
UP forward, rising, surfacing follow up on a case, back up a claim, catch up on emails
IN inward, included loop in your manager, check in with a client
BACK returning, in response get back to someone, call back a client
OFF separated, paused, cancelled hold off on shipping, write off a debt
THROUGH completing, connecting follow through on a promise, think through a decision
OVER transferring, reviewing hand over a case, look over a document
In Renata's words
"Once I decided to flag it, I had to loop Diego in before I could get a replacement out. He asked me to follow up once it shipped, and to get back to him if anything changed."
03Fill in the Particle

Dez frases, uma partícula faltando em cada. Pensa no "sentimento" dela antes de responder.

01
I need to loop ______ Diego before I respond to this one.
in — "loop in" someone means include them in the conversation.
02
We'll get a replacement shipment ______ within 48 hours.
out — "get something out" means dispatch it, send it on its way.
03
Can you follow ______ on this again next week?
up — "follow up" means check back on something later.
04
I'll get ______ to you by tomorrow morning with an answer.
back — "get back to" someone means respond to them.
05
Let's hold ______ on shipping until the account is settled.
off — "hold off" means pause or delay something.
06
She always follows ______ on the promises she makes to clients.
through — "follow through" means actually complete what you said you'd do.
07
I'm going to hand this case ______ to the collections team.
over — "hand over" means transfer responsibility to someone else.
08
We need to sort ______ this issue before the client escalates it further.
out — "sort out" means resolve or organize a messy situation.
09
He didn't check ______ with me before promising the client a discount.
in — "check in" means confirm with someone before acting.
10
Can you back ______ that claim with the actual policy clause?
up — "back up" a claim means support it with evidence.
