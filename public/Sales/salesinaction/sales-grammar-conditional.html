What If
You Switched?

Stopping a client from thinking only about price often means asking them to imagine something first. That's exactly what the second conditional is built for.

The Rule

Ask them to imagine, don't just quote a price

If + past simple, ... would + base verb. It doesn't mean the situation is likely, or unlikely. It means: let's imagine it together, for a moment.

The structure
if + past, would

The "if" part uses the past simple, but it is not about the past. The result part uses "would" plus the base verb.

"If your current supplier raised prices next quarter, would you have another option ready?"
"If we matched their price today, what would happen to the technical support you're used to?"
Why sales people use it
a question, not a threat

Second conditional lets Diego shift a conversation away from price without arguing. It's an invitation to imagine, not a warning.

"If the cheaper supplier missed a delivery, how much would that delay cost your line?"
"If you added the UV absorber, would the yellowing problem finally go away?"
The one thing that trips people up

The verb right after "if" looks like the past, but it isn't talking about the past at all. "If your supplier raised prices" is imagining next quarter, not describing something that already happened. That mismatch, past form, future or hypothetical meaning, is exactly what makes this structure feel strange the first few times. Once you accept that "if + past" is just the grammar's way of marking "this is imagined," it stops being confusing.

Vocabulary
Glossary
Diego's Two Roles
hunter
caçador
A salesperson who looks for brand new clients he has never sold to before.
Example: Diego is acting as a hunter when he cold-emails Vantec Industrial for the first time.
farmer
cultivador
A salesperson who takes care of and grows the accounts he already has.
Example: As a farmer, Diego checks in with Ravenna Coatings from time to time, even with no problem to solve.
The Pipeline
prospect (n. / v.)
cliente em potencial / prospectar
A company that could become a client but has not bought yet, or the act of searching for one.
Example: Solen Plastics is still just a prospect. Diego has not sold them anything yet.
lead
contato inicial
A potential client who has shown some interest or contact.
Example: The conversation with Rogério at the trade fair became a lead worth following up on.
pipeline
funil de vendas
The set of deals a salesperson is currently working, from first contact to close.
Example: Diego has four accounts in his pipeline this month, two new and two existing.
qualify a lead
qualificar um lead
To check whether a potential client is a real fit before investing time.
Example: Before Diego spends more time on Solen, he needs to qualify the lead and confirm they have a real problem.
buying signal
sinal de compra
Something a client says or does that suggests they are ready to buy.
Example: When Eduardo asked about delivery times, that was a clear buying signal.
close the deal
fechar negócio
To complete a sale successfully.
Example: Diego hopes to close the deal with Vantec before the end of the quarter.
proposal
proposta
A formal written offer describing what you would sell and why it fits the client's needs.
Example: Diego's proposal to Solen named the exact product, the price, and the reason it solved Rogério's problem.
pitch
apresentação de vendas
The spoken explanation of why your product or proposal is the right choice.
Example: Diego asked for 20 minutes to pitch the proposal properly, not just send it by email.
quote
orçamento / cotação
The specific price and terms you are offering for a deal.
Example: The quote included technical support, not just the price per kilo.
Requests & Orders
RFQ (Request for Quotation)
pedido de cotação
A formal request asking suppliers for pricing on a specific product or service.
Example: Eduardo sent an RFQ to three suppliers after a competitor undercut Diego's price.
RFP (Request for Proposal)
pedido de proposta
A formal request asking suppliers to propose a solution, not just a price.
Example: Solen never sent a formal RFP, Rogério just called Diego directly after the discovery conversation.
RFI (Request for Information)
pedido de informação
An early request to understand what is available in the market, before asking for prices.
Example: Some clients start with an RFI just to see which suppliers exist before requesting quotes.
purchase order (PO)
pedido de compra
The official document a client sends to confirm a purchase, including quantity, price, and terms.
Example: Rogério sent a purchase order for 200 kg of Flame Retardant Compound.
order confirmation
confirmação de pedido
The seller's reply confirming they received and accept the purchase order.
Example: Diego sent an order confirmation the same day Solen's PO arrived.
invoice
fatura / nota fiscal
The bill sent after the order ships, requesting payment.
Example: The invoice for Solen's order goes out once the Flame Retardant Compound ships.
The People
decision maker
tomador de decisão
The person who has the authority to approve a purchase.
Example: Camila is the decision maker at Vantec. Nothing moves forward without her approval.
gatekeeper
intermediário / "porteiro"
A person, often an assistant, who controls access to the decision maker.
Example: Diego had to get past the gatekeeper before he could even speak to the decision maker.
incumbent supplier
fornecedor atual
The supplier a client is currently buying from.
Example: Vantec's incumbent supplier just raised prices, which opened the door for Diego.
Your Portfolio
book of business
carteira de clientes
The group of active accounts a salesperson personally manages. The term a native speaker actually uses.
Example: Diego's book of business includes two long-term clients and two active prospects.
account portfolio
carteira de contas
A more formal version of the same idea, common in reports and reviews.
Example: The sales manager reviews everyone's account portfolio once a month.
key account
conta-chave
A client important enough to receive special attention, due to volume or strategic value.
Example: Ravenna Coatings is a key account. Losing them would hurt the whole territory.
at-risk account
conta em risco
An existing client likely to leave, often due to price or service issues.
Example: Ravenna became an at-risk account the moment a competitor undercut the price.
churn
perda de clientes
The rate at which clients stop buying from a company.
Example: Some churn among commodity clients is normal. The goal is protecting the key accounts.
Growing Accounts
cross-sell
venda cruzada
Selling a different product to a client who already buys something else.
Example: Selling a UV absorber to a client who already buys tackifiers is a cross-sell.
upsell
venda de upgrade
Selling a better, higher-value version of a product the client already buys.
Example: Diego's upsell moved Delta Adhesives from a standard tackifier to a premium one, same category, better performance.
Raw Materials
resin
resina
A base material used in paints, coatings, and adhesives.
Example: Ravenna Coatings buys a large volume of resin every month.
solvent
solvente
A liquid used to dissolve or thin another chemical product.
Example: Vantec Industrial uses several types of solvent in its repaint process.
tackifier
agente adesivante
An additive that makes an adhesive stickier.
Example: Delta Adhesives currently buys only standard tackifiers.
Product Performance
formulation
formulação
The exact combination of ingredients that makes up a chemical product.
Example: Rogério is having trouble with one specific formulation, not the whole product line.
flame retardant
retardante de chama
A chemical additive that makes a material burn more slowly.
Example: Solen Plastics needs a flame retardant that meets a new safety standard.
UV absorber
absorvedor UV
An additive that protects a product from damage caused by sunlight.
Example: The new UV absorber protects the coating from sunlight damage.
UV yellowing
amarelamento por UV
When a material slowly turns yellow after too much sun exposure.
Example: UV yellowing was ruining one of Delta's product lines.
Market Position
specialty chemical
produto químico de especialidade
A chemical product built for one specific technical job, sold on performance rather than price.
Example: A specialty chemical is priced on performance, not on volume.
commodity chemical
produto químico padrão
A standardized chemical product, largely interchangeable between suppliers, usually sold on price.
Example: A commodity chemical is easy to replace with a cheaper option.
This lesson's verbs
Verb Bank
switch
trocar / mudar
To stop using one thing and start using another.
Example: Diego hopes Eduardo won't switch suppliers over a small price difference.
see
ver
To notice or understand something clearly.
Example: If Camila compared the quotes, she would see the real difference.
explain
explicar
To describe something so it is easy to understand.
Example: Rogério needs to explain the formulation problem in more detail.
notice
notar / perceber
To become aware of something.
Example: Juliana would notice the difference after trying the new product.
lower
baixar / diminuir
To reduce something, usually a price or a number.
Example: Diego doesn't want to lower the price just to keep the account.
lose
perder
To no longer have something, like trust, an account, or money.
Example: If a delivery is missed, a client can lose trust in a supplier fast.
have
ter
To own or possess something.
Example: If Vantec had only one supplier, they would save time.
waste
desperdiçar
To use something, like time or material, without getting real value from it.
Example: An unsolved formulation problem means Solen would waste raw material.
add
adicionar
To include something new alongside what already exists.
Example: Adding the UV absorber would stop the yellowing problem.
feel
sentir
To experience an emotion or reaction.
Example: Too many calls in one week can make a client feel pressured.
sign
assinar
To write your name on a document to make it official.
Example: Signing with the cheapest supplier isn't always the safest choice.
know
saber
To have information or understanding about something.
Example: Testing the formulation would let Rogério's team know the real cause.
ask
pedir / perguntar
To request something, or to request information.
Example: Camila can always ask Diego for a sample before deciding.
remember
lembrar
To keep something in your memory, or bring it back to mind.
Example: Diego wants to remember to follow up after Juliana mentions a problem.
ignore
ignorar
To notice something but choose not to react to it.
Example: Ignoring an objection is the fastest way to lose an account.
agree
concordar
To have the same opinion as someone else.
Example: If the numbers are clear, Vantec's team is more likely to agree.
renew
renovar
To continue a contract or agreement for another period.
Example: Ravenna might renew the contract if the price stays fair.
choose
escolher
To decide on one option among several.
Example: Choosing a new supplier means requalifying the whole process.
share
compartilhar
To give someone access to information you have.
Example: Trust is what makes a client share their real budget.
need
precisar
To require something in order to do or achieve something else.
Example: Juliana would need proof of quality if her client complained.
