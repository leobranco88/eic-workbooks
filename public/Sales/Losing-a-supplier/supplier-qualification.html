Before you sign anything

Qualifying a supplier is due diligence, not paperwork.

Choosing a supplier based on price alone is how companies end up with the exact problems we've already studied — missed shipments, broken promises, and suppliers who simply can't deliver. A proper qualification process catches most of that before you sign anything.

Six things to check

These are the areas procurement teams evaluate before approving a new supplier. Each one comes with the language used to talk about it professionally.

01
Quality certifications & compliance
Can they prove they meet the standard?

Before anything else, buyers confirm that a supplier holds the certifications required for the industry, and that their processes actually follow those standards in practice, not just on paper.

"Please provide current quality certifications and confirm that your facility meets industry standards for this product category."
Key expressions
quality certification meet industry standards regulatory compliance
02
Financial stability
Can they survive a slow quarter?

A supplier's financial health determines whether they can reliably fulfill large or long-term orders without sudden disruptions. Buyers often request financial statements or run independent checks before committing.

"As part of our vetting process, we run a credit check on every new supplier to confirm financial stability before signing a long-term contract."
Key expressions
financial stability credit check cash flow issues
03
Production capacity & scalability
Can they grow with you?

A supplier might handle your current order easily but struggle the moment volume increases. Buyers assess whether current production capacity can meet demand today, and scale if the relationship grows.

"We need to understand your production capacity today, and whether you could scale up production if our order volume doubles next year."
Key expressions
production capacity scale up production meet demand
04
Track record & references
What do their other clients say?

A supplier's history with other clients reveals patterns that a sales pitch never will. Buyers ask for references and look closely for anything that seems inconsistent or evasive.

"Could you share two client references from the past year? A strong track record with similar-sized companies would be ideal."
Key expressions
track record client references red flags
05
Communication & responsiveness
How do they behave before there's a crisis?

How a supplier communicates during the qualification process often predicts how they'll behave later, under pressure. Slow or vague answers now are rarely a good sign.

"Part of our due diligence is simply observing responsiveness — how quickly and clearly a supplier answers our questions during this process."
Key expressions
responsiveness turnaround time due diligence
06
Pricing & commercial terms
What's the full deal, not just the price?

Price matters, but so do the terms surrounding it: payment conditions, minimum order sizes, and whether the supplier is willing to start with a smaller trial before a full commitment.

"Before we commit to volume, we'd like to place a trial order and confirm your standard payment terms and minimum order quantity."
Key expressions
payment terms minimum order quantity trial order

01
Before requesting samples, the buyer asked for the supplier's current ______ for food-grade packaging.
quality certification — official proof that a supplier follows recognized quality standards.
02
Every candidate supplier must show documented evidence that their facility ______ for this product.
meets industry standards — follows the accepted rules and benchmarks of the sector.
03
Legal flagged the contract until the supplier could confirm full ______ with import regulations.
regulatory compliance — meeting the legal and regulatory requirements that apply.
04
Before signing a three-year contract, the buyer wanted proof of the supplier's ______.
financial stability — a solid financial position, unlikely to collapse or default.
05
As part of the vetting process, procurement ran a ______ on the new supplier.
credit check — a formal review of a company's ability to pay its debts.
06
Late payments to their own suppliers suggested the company might be having ______.
cash flow issues — trouble with money coming in and going out on time.
07
The factory tour was mainly to confirm the supplier's real ______, not just what was on paper.
production capacity — the maximum amount a supplier can realistically produce.
08
The buyer asked whether the supplier could ______ if the order size doubled next year.
scale up production — to increase output significantly when demand grows.
09
A supplier that can barely ______ today is a risky choice for a growing company.
meet demand — to produce or deliver enough to satisfy what buyers need.
10
A supplier's ______ with similar-sized clients told us more than their sales pitch did.
track record — a supplier's history of performance with previous clients.
11
We asked for two ______ from companies of a similar size to ours.
client references — other clients a supplier can point to as proof of good service.
12
Vague answers about ownership and unclear pricing were both ______ during the interview.
red flags — warning signs that something may be wrong.
13
Slow, vague replies during qualification often predict poor ______ later, under real pressure.
responsiveness — how quickly and clearly someone reacts to a request.
14
The supplier's average ______ on emails was under four hours, which impressed the buyer.
turnaround time — the time it takes to respond or complete a task.
15
Proper ______ before signing saves companies from expensive surprises later.
due diligence — careful investigation before making a business decision.
16
The two companies still needed to agree on standard ______ before the first invoice.
payment terms — the agreed conditions for when and how payment happens.
17
Some suppliers won't accept an order below their ______, which can be a problem for smaller buyers.
minimum order quantity — the smallest amount a supplier is willing to sell per order.
18
Before committing to full volume, the buyer placed a small ______ to test quality and reliability.
trial order — a small first order used to test a new supplier before a full commitment.
