When the Customer Calls — handling delays with care

Juliana works Customer Service at Torres Beverages — the same company where Fatima was fighting the 2021 shipping crisis from the supply side. While Fatima chased containers and vendors, Juliana was the one on the phone, explaining the delay to clients who just wanted their order. This is the language of managing an upset customer without losing your cool — or the relationship.

Juliana · Customer Service, Torres Beverages
01Managing Expectations

A parte que evita a maior parte das ligações bravas: avisar antes que o cliente precise perguntar.

keep [someone] posted — send updates before they have to ask for one
manage expectations — be honest about timing instead of promising the best case
reach out proactively — contact the customer before they contact you
a realistic timeline — a date you can actually stand behind
flag a delay — raise the issue early, before it becomes a surprise
worst-case scenario — useful to mention so the real outcome feels better
In Juliana's words
"The moment Fatima's team flagged a delay on the container shipment, I didn't wait for clients to call me — I reached out proactively. I'd rather manage expectations early with a realistic timeline than promise something I can't control and disappoint them later."
02De-escalating a Frustrated Customer

Quando o aviso não foi suficiente e o cliente já está do outro lado da linha, bravo.

I completely understand your frustration — validate the feeling before defending the situation
I hear you — short, genuine, shows you're actually listening
let me look into that — a concrete next step, not just an apology
I appreciate your patience — acknowledges the wait without over-apologizing
turn this around — fix a bad experience, not just the shipment
go the extra mile — do a little more than what's strictly required
In Juliana's words
"When a client is angry, the worst thing you can do is get defensive. I hear you, I completely understand your frustration — that comes first, always. Then let me look into that, so they know something is actually happening. If we're the ones who dropped the ball, I try to go the extra mile to turn it around."
03The Call

Um cliente liga bravo por causa do atraso — o mesmo atraso que a Fatima estava tentando resolver do outro lado.

Phone Call · Juliana & Marcus (client, a distributor)
MarcusThis is the third time my order has been delayed. I have clients asking me where their stock is, and I have no answer for them.
JulianaMarcus, I completely understand your frustration — three delays in a row is not okay, and I'm not going to pretend it is.
MarcusSo what's actually happening? I keep getting vague answers.
JulianaFair. Here's the real situation: there's a global container shortage affecting the whole industry, not just us. Let me look into that shipment specifically and call you back within the hour with a realistic timeline — not a guess.
MarcusOkay. I appreciate you being straight with me, at least.
JulianaI appreciate your patience, Marcus. And going forward, I'll reach out proactively the moment anything changes — you won't have to chase me for updates again.
04Now You Try: De-escalating Tension

Três clientes, três momentos de tensão. Pra cada um, toque nas três respostas pra ver qual é a mais profissional — e por quê.

Scenario 1
"This is the third delay this month! I don't even know why I bother ordering from you anymore."
How should Juliana respond?
Version A
"Oh no, I'm SO sorry, this is terrible, I feel awful about this, please don't be upset with us..."
Too passive. Over-apologizing and begging for the customer's understanding puts the emotional weight on Juliana, not on solving the problem.
Version B
"I hear you — three delays in one month would frustrate me too. Let me pull up your account and figure out exactly what's going on."
This is the balanced response. It validates the frustration in one sentence, then moves straight into action — no over-apologizing, no defensiveness.
Version C
"To be fair, most of these delays are outside our control — it's the whole industry, not just us."
Too defensive. Even if it's true, leading with an excuse before acknowledging the customer's frustration makes it sound like you're minimizing the problem.
Scenario 2
"You promised this would ship last week. Now it's 'still processing'? What does that even mean?"
How should Juliana respond?
Version A
"Um, I'm not totally sure, let me check with someone and maybe get back to you sometime today..."
Too passive. "Not totally sure" and "maybe" make Juliana sound unprepared, and the customer is left with no real timeline at all.
Version B
"Good question — let me check the exact status right now instead of giving you a vague answer, and I'll explain what's actually happening."
This is the balanced response. It names the real issue — a vague answer isn't good enough — and commits to something concrete right away.
Version C
"It means it's still processing. These things take time, there's not much I can tell you beyond that."
Too dismissive. Repeating the same vague phrase back at the customer, with no offer to look further, will only escalate the tension.
Scenario 3
"Honestly, I'm done. I'm calling your competitor after this."
How should Juliana respond?
Version A
"Please don't go, please, I'll do literally anything, just give us one more chance, I'm begging you..."
Too passive. Begging shifts the power entirely to the customer and can come across as unprofessional, even if the intention is good.
Version B
"I don't want to lose your business over something we can fix. Give me five minutes to make this right before you decide."
This is the balanced response. It's confident, specific ("five minutes"), and focused on action — not on convincing the customer emotionally to stay.
Version C
"That's completely your call, but I doubt they'll treat you any differently given what's going on right now."
Too aggressive. Implying the competitor won't be any better sounds like a threat or a dare, not customer service — and can push the customer to leave just to prove a point.
