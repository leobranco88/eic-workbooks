The 2021 Shipping Crisis — Fatima's playbook

Fatima has run supply chain operations at Torres Beverages for eleven years. When the 2021 global shipping crisis hit — container shortages, the Suez Canal blockage, freight rates that tripled almost overnight — she was the one who kept production running. This is how she talks about it: practical, fast-paced, always pointing at the next fix.

01The Crisis

O vocabulário pra descrever o que realmente aconteceu em 2021 — passe o mouse nas expressões pra ver a tradução.

port congestion — ships stuck waiting outside overloaded ports
container shortage — not enough equipment to move the cargo that needed to move
freight rates skyrocketed — shipping costs shot up almost overnight
bottleneck — a single point that slows down the entire chain
the Suez Canal blockage — one ship, six days, a huge share of world trade stopped
lead times tripled — orders that used to take weeks started taking months
keep pace with demand — production growing as fast as what people actually need
In Fatima's words
"Container prices went from two thousand dollars to twenty thousand in a matter of months. Ships were stuck outside the port for weeks — that's port congestion at its worst. Everyone was fighting over the same equipment, and if you didn't already have a relationship with your carrier, you were at the back of the line. Most of the world's containers are made in China, and factories simply couldn't keep pace with demand — so even once the ports cleared up, there still weren't enough boxes to go around."
02How She Responded

O que a Fatima e o time da Torres Beverages realmente mudaram depois de 2021.

backup supplier — the one rule Fatima never breaks now
diversify vendors — spread the risk across more than one source
build in a buffer — keep extra stock on hand for exactly this situation
absorb the cost — take the hit yourself instead of passing it on right away
nearshoring — moving production or sourcing closer to home
long-term contracts — locking in terms instead of renegotiating in a panic
In Fatima's words
"I always have a backup supplier for anything critical — that's the rule I never break now. We diversified vendors across three countries instead of one, built in a buffer of extra inventory for our top ten SKUs, and signed long-term contracts so we weren't renegotiating every single shipment at panic prices."
03Looking Back

Uma entrevista curta de podcast — o jeito prático e direto da Fatima de falar sobre o assunto.

Podcast Interview · Diego & Fatima (Torres Beverages)
DiegoTake me back to 2021. What was the actual day-to-day like?
FatimaChaos, honestly. Every shipment was a gamble. Lead time went from six weeks to six months on some routes. I was checking customs updates every morning before coffee.
DiegoWhat's the one habit that came out of that year and never left?
FatimaNever rely on a single vendor for anything critical. I don't care how good the relationship is — if they go down, you go down with them.
DiegoIf another crisis hit tomorrow, would you be ready?
FatimaReadier than in 2021, for sure. We've got backup suppliers, we've got buffer inventory, and we've got contracts that protect us on price. Nobody's ever fully ready — but we wouldn't be improvising this time.
