The Batch That Didn't Pass — a quality complaint from start to finish

Beatriz picked up the sample jar the moment it arrived from receiving. A quick visual inspection usually tells her enough — but not this time. The powder looked slightly clumped, which almost always means one thing: moisture. She didn't wait to find out the hard way. She sent it straight to the lab and quarantined the batch until the results came back.
Teacher's Note · Pronunciation
"Moisture" trips up a lot of Portuguese speakers. That "oi" sounds like the "oy" in "boy" — /ˈmɔɪs·tʃɚ/, not "mo-ees-ture." "Moisture" engana quem fala português — o "oi" soa como o "oy" de "boy", não como "mo-ees-tiur".
And "clumped" just means stuck together in small lumps, instead of staying loose and separate — the opposite of "free-flowing" (more on that in Phase 2). "Clumped" significa empelotado, grudado em pequenas bolinhas — o oposto de "free-flowing".
Mission: say these three words out loud, stressing the first syllable — MOISture, CLUMPed, SAMple.
Why did Beatriz send the sample to the lab instead of just trusting her own inspection?
Because clumping can be a sign of a moisture problem that isn't fully visible without lab testing — she needed the actual numbers, not just a visual impression.

The lab results confirmed it: moisture content came back at 2.8%, well above the 1.5% tolerance range specified in the contract. Beatriz compared the number against Kessler's own Certificate of Analysis — which claimed 1.2% for that same lot number. Someone's numbers were wrong. She filed a non-conformance report and requested a root-cause investigation before accepting any explanation over the phone.
Teacher's Note · Reading Numbers
In English, decimals use a point, never a comma. 1.5% is said "one point five percent" — not "one comma five." Em inglês, decimais usam ponto, nunca vírgula. O que em português é "1,5%" vira "one point five percent" em inglês — nunca "one comma five".
The symbol ≤ is read "no more than" or "at most." So "≤ 1.5%" means "no more than one point five percent." O símbolo "≤" se lê "no more than" ou "at most" (no máximo).
"Free-flowing" describes a powder that pours and moves easily, without sticking together. Its opposite is "clumped" or "caked." "Free-flowing" descreve um pó que escorre e se move facilmente, sem grudar. O oposto é "clumped" ou "caked".
Mission: before reading the table below, say all three moisture percentages out loud — 1.5%, 1.2%, and 2.8%. Then try this full sentence: "The requirement is no more than one point five percent moisture."

The lot number matches, so this isn't a shipping mix-up. The real gap is between Kessler's own CoA (1.2%) and the lab result (2.8%) — which means the discrepancy started on Kessler's end, before the batch even left Hamburg. That's why Beatriz asked for a root-cause investigation instead of just accepting an apology.
What did Beatriz do before accepting any explanation from Kessler?
She filed a non-conformance report (NCR) and requested a root-cause investigation — documentation first, explanation second.

With the numbers confirmed, it was time to escalate. Beatriz called Jonas, her contact at Kessler, and got straight to the point.
Phone Call · Beatriz & Jonas (Kessler Raw Materials)
BeatrizJonas, I need to escalate this. The moisture content on lot KM-4471 is almost double what your CoA states.
JonasI understand your concern, but our lab results were within range when it left Hamburg…
BeatrizI'm not looking for reassurance — I'm looking for a corrective action plan. We need a credit note for this batch and a replacement shipment by next week.
JonasI'll get you an answer by tomorrow morning.
BeatrizI'm going to hold Kessler accountable for this one — it's the second time this quarter.
Same Message, Different Tone
Beatriz could have opened the call three different ways. Tap each one to see why it works or doesn't.
Version A
"Hi Jonas, so sorry to bother you again, but I think maybe there might be a small issue with the moisture content? Whenever you have a moment, no rush at all!"
Too passive. Apologizing for raising a real quality issue, and "no rush at all" undercuts the urgency — Kessler has no reason to prioritize this.
Version B
"Jonas, I need to escalate this. The moisture content on lot KM-4471 is almost double what your CoA states. We need a corrective action plan, a credit note, and a replacement shipment by next week."
This is the one Beatriz actually used. Direct, specific (numbers, lot, deadline), and firm — without being personal or hostile. This is the professional register to aim for.
Version C
"Jonas, this is completely unacceptable. Your team clearly doesn't check anything before shipping. Fix this immediately or we're canceling the contract."
Too aggressive. Attacking the team ("clearly doesn't check anything") and jumping straight to threats can damage the relationship — and gives Jonas a reason to get defensive instead of helpful.
What two concrete things does Beatriz ask Jonas for on the call?
A credit note for the failed batch, and a replacement shipment within a week.

A week later, Kessler's regional manager, Andreas, flew in to close things out in person. Beatriz wanted more than an apology — she wanted a plan.
In-Person Meeting · Beatriz & Andreas (Kessler Raw Materials)
AndreasWe've reviewed the root cause — it was a calibration issue in our moisture sensor at the Hamburg site.
BeatrizThat explains the pattern we saw. What's the action plan going forward?
AndreasWe're recalibrating this week, and we've scheduled a follow-up audit before your next shipment leaves the facility.
BeatrizGood. I'll close the case once I see that audit report — not before.
Teacher's Note · Word Stress
Long technical words like calibration and documentation almost always stress the syllable right before "-tion": ca-li-BRA-tion, do-cu-men-TA-tion. Get that syllable right and the rest falls into place. Palavras técnicas longas terminadas em "-tion" quase sempre têm a sílaba tônica bem antes do "-tion": ca-li-BRA-tion, do-cu-men-TA-tion.
Mission: read Andreas's first line out loud, stressing calibration correctly — "it was a ca-li-BRA-tion issue in our moisture sensor."
Why doesn't Beatriz close the case right away, even after hearing the action plan?
Because a plan is a promise, not proof — she wants to see the follow-up audit report before considering the case resolved.
