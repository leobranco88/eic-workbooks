Turning Beatriz Into an Ally
How This Meeting Happened

After Renata approved the technical results last week, Diego asked her one simple question: is anyone else here dealing with the same kind of problem? Renata didn't hesitate. She mentioned Beatriz's adhesion issue right away and set up fifteen minutes for him in the R&D lab.

Prisma Coatings, R&D Lab. 10:15 AM.
Scene Context
Beatriz has been fighting an adhesion failure on the aluminum facade panels, one of Prisma Coatings' main product lines, for months. Other suppliers have offered workarounds, but never a real fix. Diego's lab results say the new formulation fixes exactly that problem. She has not agreed to anything yet, she only knows the numbers work. What she does next depends entirely on how Diego frames this.
Scene Objectives
  • Show Beatriz the results in a way that centers her problem, not Coatex's product.
  • Turn a satisfied user into someone who advocates for Coatex when Diego is not in the room.
  • Do not take credit for solving something she has been fighting for months on her own.
The Product Behind the Problem
Diego, Renata, and Beatriz are not solving an abstract issue. They are all focused on the same physical product: Prisma Coatings' aluminum facade panels. Different departments, same panel.
Diego Coatex sells the coating.
Renata Quality validates it before it reaches the line.
Beatriz R&D is the one who actually solves problems like this.
Jonas Production applies it on the line, every shift.
Amauri Production protects the line from any disruption.
Useful Language
"I think this should come from you, not me."Acho que isso deveria vir de você, não de mim.
"This is finally a real fix, not just another workaround."Isso é finalmente uma solução verdadeira, não só mais um remendo.
"I'm happy to back you up if anyone has questions."Fico feliz em te apoiar se alguém tiver perguntas.
"You know the technical story better than anyone in the room."Você conhece a história técnica melhor do que qualquer um na sala.
Talk About It
What Research Says: Sales experts have a name for what just happened in this scene. They call it building a champion. A champion is a person inside the client's company who believes in your product and speaks for it when you are not in the room. Research shows that a strong champion can matter more than the person who signs the contract.
Do you agree that a champion can matter more than the person who signs the contract? Why or why not?
What does it mean to let someone else own a win?
Why might staying quiet in the room actually help your case?
What is the difference between supporting a champion and taking over their moment?
Discuss with your partner or teacher before moving on.
