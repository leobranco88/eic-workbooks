Before It Breaks · Grammar

Was It
Supposed To?

A service plan is supposed to prevent breakdowns. Sometimes it does exactly that, sometimes it doesn't. The grammar doesn't tell you which, the situation does.

The Rule

Your opinion, or the plan?

"Should" is what you think is right. "Be supposed to" is what was agreed, planned, or expected by someone else, or by a contract.

Your opinion
should

Use it to give advice or say what you personally think is right. Nobody agreed to this, it's just your view.

"I think Marcelo should ask for a longer contract." (my opinion, not a rule)
The plan, the agreement
be supposed to

Use it for what was arranged, expected, or written into a contract, not your personal opinion.

"The Service Plan is supposed to cover two inspections a year." (that's what the contract says)
The trap for Portuguese speakers

In Portuguese you say "era para eu ligar". The natural instinct is to translate this word by word into "it was to call", but that is wrong, English doesn't work that way here. The correct form is "I was supposed to call." "Supposed to" is one fixed expression, it doesn't break apart the way "era para" does in Portuguese. And here's the part that trips people up even more: in the past, "was/were supposed to" often means something didn't happen as planned, but not always. Look at our own Document 05: "it did exactly what it was supposed to do", here the expectation WAS met. The grammar itself is neutral, only the situation tells you if the plan worked out or not.

Vocabulary
Glossary
Proving Value
total cost of ownership (TCO)
custo total de propriedade
The real cost of owning equipment, including repairs, downtime, and lost production, not just the purchase price.
Example: Thiago's proposal compared total cost of ownership, not just the price of the plan.
objection
objeção
A reason a client gives for not buying, that a salesperson has to respond to.
Example: Fábio's objection was that nothing had broken, so the plan felt pointless.
budget approval
aprovação de orçamento
Internal permission, usually from a manager or director, to spend money on something.
Example: Marcelo needed budget approval before he could sign anything.
The Contract
service level agreement (SLA)
acordo de nível de serviço
A contract that defines exactly what service is guaranteed, and how fast.
Example: The SLA promises a 24-hour response time on every service call.
renewal
renovação
The decision to continue a service contract when it reaches the end of its term.
Example: Patrícia's renewal is due in 30 days.
upgrade
upgrade
Moving a client to a higher, more complete service tier.
Example: Thiago proposed an upgrade from the Service Plan to the Total Responsibility Plan.
Keeping the Account
at-risk account
conta em risco
An existing client likely to leave or cancel a contract.
Example: Fábio became an at-risk account the moment he asked how to cancel.
churn
perda de clientes
The rate at which clients cancel or fail to renew a service.
Example: A little churn is normal, but not on accounts this size.
The Machine
installed base
base instalada
The group of machines a company already sold, now in use at client sites.
Example: Thiago's job is built entirely around Ventix's installed base of compressors.
running hours
horas de operação
The total number of hours a machine has actually been used, often used to schedule maintenance.
Example: Maintenance is scheduled based on running hours, not the calendar.
genuine parts
peças genuínas
Original parts made by the equipment manufacturer, instead of generic replacements.
Example: Genuine parts protect the compressor's lifespan and resale value.
Uptime & Downtime
uptime
tempo em operação
The amount of time a machine is actually running and available for use.
Example: The Total Responsibility Plan includes a guaranteed uptime target.
downtime
tempo de parada
The amount of time a machine is stopped and not producing.
Example: Downtime usually costs more than the repair itself.
breakdown
quebra / parada não planejada
A sudden equipment failure that stops production.
Example: Ferro Sul had never had a breakdown that cost more than the repair itself, until it did.
Two Kinds of Maintenance
preventive maintenance
manutenção preventiva
Scheduled maintenance performed before a problem happens, to avoid a bigger one later.
Example: The Service Plan includes preventive maintenance at fixed intervals.
corrective maintenance
manutenção corretiva
Repair work done after a machine has already broken down.
Example: Before the plan, Ferro Sul only ever paid for corrective maintenance.
response time
tempo de resposta
How quickly a technician arrives after a service call.
Example: Norvex's contract guarantees a 24-hour response time.
Contract Terms
loss of profits
lucros cessantes
The profit a client would have earned if their equipment hadn't failed. Rarely something a service company agrees to cover.
Example: Norvex's legal team wanted a loss of profits clause. Ventix's legal team said no.
actual damages
danos emergentes
The real, provable cost of a problem, not the profit that was never earned.
Example: Actual damages cover the repair itself, not what Norvex might have made if the machine hadn't stopped.
liability cap
teto de responsabilidade
The maximum amount a company agrees to be responsible for, usually written into the contract.
Example: Thiago proposed a liability cap equal to the annual contract value.
indemnity
indenização
A promise to compensate someone for a specific loss or damage.
Example: The indemnity clause only applies if Ventix's own negligence caused the damage.
force majeure
força maior
An unexpected event outside anyone's control, like a natural disaster, that excuses a company from meeting the contract.
Example: A flood that shuts down the factory would fall under force majeure, not a service failure.
