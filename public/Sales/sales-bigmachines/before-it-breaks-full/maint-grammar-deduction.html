Dedução em inglês muda de forma dependendo do momento: agora (must be) ou antes de acontecer (must have been). A certeza não muda, só a distância no tempo muda a estrutura. Hoje praticamos as duas durante uma auditoria técnica na Northwind HQ.

Warm-Up
Antes de começar
Vocabulary Note
to service a unit significa fazer manutenção num equipamento, não "atender" no sentido de atendimento ao cliente. Pra isso, o inglês usa outro verbo.
Manutenção (equipamento)
"The technician services this compressor twice a year."
O técnico faz manutenção nesse compressor duas vezes por ano.
Atendimento (cliente)
"Alex attends to the customer as soon as she arrives."
Alex atende a cliente assim que ela chega.
Same Word, Different Job
Vale reforçar: customer service (substantivo) é "atendimento ao cliente", mas o verbo pra atender uma pessoa nunca é "to service", isso é praticamente só pra máquinas e equipamentos.
⚠ Watch out
attend to e attend (sem o "to") não significam a mesma coisa. O "to" é obrigatório quando o sentido é "cuidar de, atender".
Attend to + pessoa
"Alex attends to the customer's questions."
Alex cuida das perguntas do cliente (atende).
Attend (sem "to") + evento
"Alex attends the meeting every Monday."
Alex comparece à reunião toda segunda.
attend to é meio formal (recepção, atendimento médico). No dia a dia comercial, é mais comum ouvir help a customer (o mais neutro), assist a customer (mais formal), serve a customer (varejo/restaurante), ou wait on a customer (garçom, vendedor de loja).

01
The Walkthrough
Leitura · told by Alex Morgan, Sales Engineer
Northwind HQ · Plant Floor · a routine technical audit
Act 1 · Right now
Carlos · the ears

Carlos leads the way past Line 4. Even from ten feet away, I can hear a low grinding sound. It's coming from the motor housing. Carlos doesn't look worried, just curious. "That must be a bearing," he says. He taps the casing with two fingers. "Or it could be something looser. Maybe a bolt."

Alex · the panel

I check the gauge on the panel next to it. The temperature reading is very high. That's strange for a unit running at half load. "This can't be right," I say. I point at the number.

We keep walking. Near the back wall, there's a strange smell. It's not smoke, but something smells burnt. Carlos notices it too. "Might be the coupling," he says. "Or it could be old grease, burning off slowly." Two ideas, no clear answer yet. We don't want to guess too soon.

Act 2 · Looking back
Emma · the log

Back in the control room, Emma Brooks opens the maintenance log. "Let's check what happened," she says. She scrolls back three weeks. Her finger stops on a gap in the schedule. "This must have started earlier than today," she says.

The numbers show something unexpected. "Someone must have skipped the last lubrication cycle," Emma says. "It should have run in week six. It didn't." I ask if the extra load from last month could have caused the problem instead. "It might have contributed," she says. "But the gap is the real clue. This can't have been just normal wear and tear. Wear and tear happens slowly. It doesn't create an empty gap like this in the schedule."

I stood there with Carlos and Emma, and something surprised me. We all read the evidence in a different way. Carlos trusted the sound. Emma trusted the log. I trusted the number on the panel. Three clues, one answer: the compressor didn't fail today. It had been failing for weeks. Today was just the day we finally noticed.

Deduction · Present
O que provavelmente é verdade agora
Quase certeza
must be
~95% de certeza

Você tem evidência forte. A conclusão é quase inevitável.

"The panel is dark. The sensor must be disconnected."

Possibilidade
might / may be
~40 a 60% de certeza

Uma possibilidade real, sem evidência forte de nenhum lado.

"It might be the coupling. Hard to say yet."

Possibilidade
could be
~40 a 60% de certeza

Quase igual a "might be". Outra opção em aberto.

"That could be a loose bolt, or something else."

Quase impossível
can't be
~95% de impossibilidade

A evidência torna a ideia praticamente impossível.

"This can't be right. Not for a unit at half load."

A regra de ouro: nunca use "mustn't be" para dedução negativa. Para dizer que algo é quase impossível, use can't be.
Dica extra
Esses modais de dedução vêm seguidos de be + adjetivo ou substantivo, ou de um verbo com -ing quando descrevem uma ação em progresso: "It must be overheating right now." A estrutura de certeza não muda, só o verbo depois muda de forma.
Certainty Scale · Agora
can't be
might be / could be
must be
impossível → possível → quase certo
Evidência forte → must be

The panel light is completely dark and the needle hasn't moved in ten minutes, even though the line is clearly running.

The sensor must be disconnected.

Por quê "must be": a evidência (luz apagada, agulha parada, linha rodando) é forte o suficiente para uma conclusão quase certa. Não é opinião, é a leitura mais lógica dos fatos.

Evidência contraditória → can't be

The maintenance log clearly shows this exact bearing was replaced two weeks ago, but Carlos is pointing straight at it as the source of the sound.

This can't be the same bearing failing again already.

Por quê "can't be": o log contradiz a suposição. Quando algo é logicamente improvável dado o que você vê nos dados, é "can't be", nunca "mustn't be".

03
Deduction · Past
O que provavelmente aconteceu antes
Quase certeza sobre o passado
must have been
~95% de certeza

Você olha para trás e a conclusão é quase inevitável.

"There's a gap in the log. Someone must have skipped it."

Possibilidade sobre o passado
might / could have been
~40 a 60% de certeza

Uma explicação possível para algo que já aconteceu.

"The extra load might have contributed. Hard to be sure."

Possibilidade sobre o passado
may have been
~40 a 60% de certeza

Mesma força de "might have". Mais formal, comum em relatórios.

"The delay may have been caused by a parts shortage."

Quase impossível no passado
can't have been
~95% de impossibilidade

A evidência torna essa versão do passado praticamente impossível.

"This can't have been wear and tear. It doesn't skip weeks."

Formando o passado: modal + have + particípio passado. "Must have skipped", "could have caused", "can't have been". O verbo depois de "have" nunca muda de forma.
Certainty Scale · Antes
can't have been
might / could have been
must have been
impossível → possível → quase certo
⚠ Watch out
"could" sozinho no passado, sem "have", quase sempre significa habilidade geral, não dedução. O "have" é o que muda tudo.
Habilidade
"Carlos could fix any compressor blindfolded."
Ele era capaz de consertar, sabia fazer isso.
Dedução
"He could have fixed it before the shift ended."
Talvez ele tenha consertado, não temos certeza.
Conclusão forte sobre o passado

The log shows a three week gap exactly where the lubrication cycle was scheduled to run.

Someone must have skipped the last cycle.

Por quê "must have skipped": a evidência (o intervalo exato no cronograma) torna essa a explicação quase certa para algo que já aconteceu. Presente da evidência, passado da ação.

Impossibilidade sobre o passado

The technician insists it was normal wear, but wear and tear happens gradually, it doesn't create a three week gap in the schedule.

This can't have been just wear and tear.

Por quê "can't have been": a lógica dos dados contradiz a explicação. Impossibilidade sobre o passado é sempre "can't have", nunca "mustn't have".

The extreme cold extra strain on the seals.

Asking & Answering
Como perguntar e responder usando dedução
Yes/No"Could this be a sensor issue?"Modal no início, como qualquer pergunta de sim/não.
Yes/No · past"Might they have skipped the inspection?"Modal + subject + have + particípio.
Wh-question"What could be causing the noise?"Wh-word antes do modal.
Wh · past"Why might the bearing have failed early?"Wh-word + modal + subject + have + particípio.
Short answer"It could be, yes." · "Probably not, it can't be." · "It must have been, I think."Resposta curta, sem repetir a frase toda.
Exemplo resolvido · pergunta + resposta curta

The temperature panel on Line 4 shows a reading well above normal, even though the unit is only running at half load.

Q: "What could be causing this?"
A: "It must be a blocked cooling line. That's the only thing that explains a jump this big."

Por quê funciona: a pergunta usa "could be" porque você ainda não sabe a causa, só quer investigar. A resposta usa "must be" porque agora você está dando sua conclusão mais provável, com confiança.

Discussion · 4 questions

In the reading, Carlos trusts the sound, Emma trusts the log, and Alex trusts the number on the panel. If you could only pick one of those three sources of evidence on a real plant floor, which would you pick, and why?

Emma writes "must have skipped" in her notes with Alex standing right there, but a formal report going to Richard Hall might use "may have been skipped" instead. What changes between those two situations?

Richard Hall calls Alex the next morning asking why nobody caught this earlier. Using only what's in the reading, give Alex three different explanations, one with must have, one with might have, and one with could have.

The module's central question is "How do you prove something is worth it, before it breaks?" What does deduction, reading clues before you have proof, have to do with answering that question?
