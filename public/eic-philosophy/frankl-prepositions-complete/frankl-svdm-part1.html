Same Verb,
Different Meaning

Until now, the preposition only organized the sentence. Here, it changes the verb's entire meaning. "Look at" and "look for" are practically two different verbs. You learn these the way you learn vocabulary: one by one.

What changed
In Dependent Prepositions, the preposition organized the sentence but the verb kept its meaning: afraid of, committed to, tired of. Here, the preposition changes the verb's entire meaning. Look at and look for are practically two different verbs. Grammars call these phrasal verbs and prepositional verbs. The difference between the two matters for one thing only: whether you can separate the verb from the preposition. The tags in this material tell you that.
Nas Dependent Prepositions, a preposição organizava a frase mas o verbo continuava com o mesmo significado: afraid of, committed to, tired of. Aqui, a preposição muda o sentido inteiro. Look at e look for são praticamente dois verbos diferentes. As gramáticas chamam isso de phrasal verbs e prepositional verbs. A diferença entre os dois importa para uma coisa: se separa ou não. As tags no material indicam isso.

01
look
6 meanings
Before you read the rule

Elena notices a student is upset. She could observe the student's work, investigate what went wrong, or take care of the student after class. English uses "look" for all three. What changes?

Frankl says we should never feel superior to someone who has not found meaning. He also says we should look forward to the challenge of responding to life. Same verb. Completely different actions.

look
at
observe something specific Elena looked at the drawing Tomás left behind. olhar para
for
search for something Yara was looking for a reason to stay at the ward. procurar
after
take care of someone She has been looking after the same patients for years. cuidar de
into
investigate, examine Elena decided to look into what the student really meant. investigar
forward to
anticipate with pleasure all registers Yara looks forward to walking into the ward every morning. ansiar por
down on
feel superior to, despise informalobrigatório Frankl never looked down on those who struggled to find meaning. desprezar

think
3 meanings
Before you read the rule

There is a difference between having an opinion about something, spending time reflecting on it, and weighing it carefully before a decision. English uses "think" for all three. How does the preposition tell you which one?

Tomás thinks about his son every day. But when someone asks "what do you think of Frankl?", they want a quick opinion, not a deep reflection. Why?

think
of
have an opinion; associate with What do you think of Frankl's argument? achar, associar
about
reflect on, spend time considering Tomás has been thinking about his son all week. pensar em
over
consider carefully before decidingseparável Elena needs to think it over before changing her approach. refletir com calma

2 meanings
Before you read the rule

Yara says two things: "I dream of opening a clinic one day" and "I dreamed about a patient last night." One points to the future, the other to the past. Which preposition points where?

dream
of
aspire to, have an ambition neutral / literary Yara dreams of building something that outlasts her. aspirar a
about

04
care
2 meanings
Before you read the rule

A hospital administrator asks Yara: "Do you care about these patients?" and "Who cares for them at night?" One is about feeling, the other about action. Yara does both. Most people only do one.

care
about
value emotionally, be invested in Elena still cares about her students, even when the work feels empty. se importar com
for
look after neutral; would you like? formal Yara cares for patients who cannot care for themselves. cuidar de; gostar (formal)
05
agree
3 meanings
Before you read the rule

Three sentences, three prepositions, three kinds of agreement: "I agree with you" (shared belief), "We agreed on a deadline" (shared decision), "She agreed to the new terms" (acceptance). Which kind of agreement happens in each?

agree
with
share the same view as someone Tomás does not always agree with Frankl, but he respects the argument. concordar com
on
reach consensus on a specific point They agreed on reading one chapter per week. concordar sobre
to
accept a proposal or condition Elena agreed to try a different approach next term. aceitar

talk
4 meanings
Before you read the rule

Yara has a patient who refuses medication. She could talk to him (open a channel), talk about the medication (discuss the subject), talk him into taking it (persuade), or talk him out of refusing (dissuade). Four prepositions, four completely different outcomes.

talk
to
have a conversation with Elena needs to talk to the student who asked the question. conversar com
about
discuss a subject They talked about Frankl for over an hour. falar sobre
into
persuade someone to do something informalobrigatório Yara talked him into staying one more week. convencer a fazer
out of
dissuade someone from doing something informalobrigatório Elena talked her colleague out of quitting mid-term. convencer a não fazer
07
wait
3 meanings
Before you read the rule

"Wait for" is patience. "Wait on" is dependency. Frankl would say: do not wait for meaning to arrive. Do not wait on someone else to give you a reason. Go and respond to what life is already asking.

wait
for
expect someone or something to arrive Tomás waited for the feeling of purpose to return. It never came by waiting. esperar por
on
depend on something external informal AmE; serve neutral We cannot wait on meaning to appear. Frankl says we must respond first. depender de; servir (garçom)
up
stay awake waiting for someone informal Elena waited up for her daughter after the late study group. ficar acordado esperando
