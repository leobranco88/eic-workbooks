02 · With a Subject — proud of her trying
YaraI'm proud of Elena trying something different with her class.
ElenaAre you surprised at me admitting it did not work?
YaraA little. I'm used to you being harder on yourself.
TomásI am not accustomed to people noticing when something changes in the work.
ElenaWe are all proud of you keeping going.
The Rule
preposition + [subject] + gerund → proud of her trying

When the gerund has its own subject, place it immediately before the gerund. In speech, use the object pronoun (her, him, them). In formal writing, use the possessive (her, his, their). Both are acceptable.

03 · Negatives — preposition + not + gerund
ElenaI am afraid of not finding my way back into the classroom with purpose.
TomásI am tired of not being able to explain why I keep working.
YaraI used to worry about not saying the right thing. Now I worry about not being really there.
TomásI am proud of Yara not pretending she has answers when she does not.
The Rule
preposition + not + gerund → afraid of not finding

To negate the gerund, place not directly before it — not before the preposition. The not belongs to the action. You can combine subject and negative in the same structure.

Changes the meaning entirely She isn't worried about finding the answer.
(she does not worry at all)
What you actually mean She is worried about not finding the answer.
(she worries — about failing to find)
Subject + not + gerund together: proud of her not giving up — she is the one not giving up.

04 · Perfect Gerund — having + past participle
ElenaI am proud of having changed how I approach the first lesson of the year.
TomásI sometimes think about not having spoken more while my son was alive.
YaraI am grateful for having found this work — even late.
YaraI am tired of not having said certain things when there was still time.
The Rule
preposition + having + past participle → proud of having found

Use having + past participle when the gerund action happened before the main verb. To negate: not having + past participle. To add a subject: her having found, him not having spoken.

Simple gerund She is proud of finding her place.
general — no time reference
Perfect gerund She is proud of having found her place.
the finding already happened — before the pride
All three layers together: subject + not + having + past participle.
He is ashamed of her not having been told the truth sooner.
