    PatternFrameDica
    want + sb + toI want my team to + verb
    I want my reps to call every customer this week.
    		Depois da pessoa vem “to” + verbo. Negativo: “I don’t want him to …”.
    ask + sb + toI asked the customer to + verb
    I asked the customer to send the purchase order today.
    		Pedir que alguém faça algo. Negativo: “asked him not to …”.
    tell + sb + toI told the technician to + verb
    I told the technician to call me before he left the plant.
    		Dar uma ordem ou uma instrução. Negativo: “told her not to …”.
    advise + sb + toI advised them to + verb
    I advised the customer to sign a longer contract.
    		Precisa da pessoa antes do “to”. Nunca “advise to do”.
    remind + sb + toI reminded her to + verb
    I reminded the team to update the CRM on Friday.
    		Lembrar alguém de fazer. A pessoa vem antes do “to”.
    expect + sb + toI expect my reps to + verb
    I expect the customer to renew before the contract expires.
    		Esperar que alguém faça. Depois da pessoa: “to” + verbo.
    let + sb + verbI let him + verb
    I let the customer choose the date of the visit.
    		Permitir. Sem “to”. Negativo: “I didn’t let him …”.
    make + sb + verbThe delay made them + verb
    The breakdown made them stop the whole line.
    		Forçar ou causar. Sem “to”. Passado: “made”.
    have + sb + verbI had the team + verb
    I had my team revise the proposal before lunch.
    		Pedir que alguém faça (um serviço). Sem “to”.
    suggest + that + sb + verbI suggested that he + verb
    I suggested that he postpone the shutdown to a weekend.
    		Depois de “that”: verbo simples. Sem “to” e sem “-s”.
    recommend + that + sb + verbI recommended that they + verb
    I recommended that she call the customer today.
    		Igual a “suggest”. Verbo simples, mesmo com “he” ou “she”.
    insist + that + sb + verbHe insisted that we + verb
    The customer insisted that we send a technician the same day.
    		Insistir que alguém faça. Verbo simples depois de “that”.

    How to play · como jogar

    1. Draw a situation and a verb pattern.Aperte Sortear. O aluno recebe uma situação de trabalho, o contexto e um verb pattern.
    2. Speak for 45 seconds. Use the pattern.Ele usa o pattern sorteado e pode olhar as collocations. Duas ou três já ajudam.
    3. Answer two follow-up questions.Cada pergunta empurra para um pattern diferente. Ele não pode responder com uma frase só.
    4. Score: 1 point for each pattern, 2 for the drawn one. Goal: 8.Cada pattern usado corretamente vale 1 ponto. O sorteado vale 2. A meta da rodada é 8.

    All 13 situations · as 13 situações
    1. 01
      A big customer did not renew the contract.

      A big customer did not renew his maintenance contract. He said the price was too high and that a competitor offered a cheaper plan. You are calling him next week, and your director wants to know what went wrong.

      renew a service contract let the contract expire lose a customer to a competitor offer a discount win the customer back go over the terms

      Example I asked the customer to tell me why he left. He told me to send a better offer, so I had my team prepare a new proposal.

      To start: What will you ask the customer to do when you call him next week? ask + sb + to

    2. 02
      A technician missed a scheduled visit.

      A technician did not show up for a scheduled visit at your customer’s plant. The customer is angry because the equipment has been running badly for days. You have to fix this today and keep the relationship.

      miss a scheduled visit reschedule the visit apologize for the delay send a replacement technician keep the customer informed make up for the mistake

      Example I told the technician to go to the plant right away. I asked the customer to give us one more chance, and I let him choose the new time.

      To start: What will you tell the technician to do today? tell + sb + to

    3. 03
      A customer’s equipment stopped the production line.

      The customer’s production line has been stopped for six hours. Every hour of downtime costs him money, and he is asking who is responsible. He never signed a full maintenance plan with you.

      unplanned downtime stop the production line emergency repair spare parts in stock preventive maintenance plan respond within four hours

      Example The breakdown made him lose a full day of production. I recommended that he sign a preventive plan, and I advised him to keep spare parts in stock.

      To start: What do you advise the customer to do right now? advise + sb + to

    4. 04
      A customer wants a big discount to renew.

      A customer will renew only if you cut the price by a lot. Your finance team says the margin is already low. You have to decide what to offer without losing the deal.

      negotiate the terms protect the margin match a competitor’s price add value instead of cutting the price get approval from finance close the deal

      Example I insisted that we keep the price, but I let the customer choose extra services. I asked finance to approve a small discount.

      To start: What will you suggest instead of a big discount? suggest + that

    5. 05
      A rep on your team is missing his target.

      One of your reps has missed his target for two months. He has good customers, but he forgets to follow up on proposals. You have a one-to-one meeting with him tomorrow.

      hit the quarterly target fall behind on the target follow up on a proposal review the pipeline coach a rep set clear goals

      Example I expect my reps to follow up within two days. I told him to update the CRM, and I had him show me his pipeline.

      To start: What do you want him to change before the end of the quarter? want + sb + to

    6. 06
      A competitor is offering a cheaper service.

      Three of your customers received an offer from a competitor with a lower price. The competitor’s contract is cheaper, but the response time is slower. You need to explain your value before more customers leave.

      undercut our price total cost of ownership justify the price stand out from the competition hold on to customers compare the response times

      Example I advised the customers not to choose only by price. I reminded them to count the cost of downtime, and I suggested that they compare the response times.

      To start: What do you advise your customers not to do? advise + sb + to

    7. 07
      The spare parts are delayed.

      A shipment of spare parts is stuck at customs. A customer is waiting for those parts to finish a repair, and your logistics team cannot give a date. You have to talk to the customer today.

      be stuck at customs track the shipment expedite the order a firm delivery date lead time bring the repair forward

      Example I asked logistics to track the shipment. The delay made me call the customer before he called me, and I told him not to worry.

      To start: What will you ask logistics to do today? ask + sb + to

    8. 08
      Your team must log everything in the CRM.

      Your company just started requiring every visit and every proposal in the CRM. Some reps think it wastes time. You have to explain why it matters and make sure the team follows it.

      update the CRM log a visit keep the records up to date build the sales forecast track the pipeline waste time on paperwork

      Example I want everyone to log their visits on the same day. I insist that nobody skip it, because the forecast depends on it.

      To start: What will you remind the team to log after every visit? remind + sb + to

    9. 09
      A customer wants to postpone a planned shutdown.

      A customer wants to move a planned shutdown because of a big order from his own client. If the maintenance is delayed, the risk of a breakdown grows. You have to protect the customer without causing a conflict.

      a planned shutdown postpone the maintenance the risk of a breakdown fit into the schedule work overtime sign off on the new date

      Example I advised him not to postpone it, but I let him pick a weekend. I recommended that he schedule the work at night.

      To start: Would you let him move the shutdown? Why or why not? let + sb + verb

    10. 10
      Your pipeline is thin for next quarter.

      You have few open proposals for the next quarter, and the director asks for a forecast on Monday. Several customers have not answered in weeks. You need a plan to fill the pipeline fast.

      build the pipeline qualify a lead chase a customer for an answer fall short of the forecast generate new leads prioritize key accounts

      Example I told my team to call every open proposal. I had them list the top ten accounts, and I expect them to tell me the real chances.

      To start: What will you tell your team to do first? tell + sb + to

    11. 11
      A customer asks for a stricter SLA.

      A customer wants a faster response time written in the contract (an SLA, a service level agreement), with a penalty if you fail. Operations says it is hard to promise. You have to negotiate something both sides can accept.

      a service level agreement a penalty clause commit to a deadline guarantee a response time be realistic about what we can deliver meet the terms

      Example The customer insisted that we guarantee four hours. I asked operations to check the real numbers, and I advised him to accept a smaller penalty.

      To start: What do you expect operations to say? expect + sb + to

    12. 12
      Finance rejected your proposal.

      Finance said the margin on your proposal is too low, so it cannot go to the customer. The customer is waiting, and the deadline is Friday. You need to find a way to save the deal.

      reject a proposal the margin is too low revise the numbers meet the deadline escalate to the director reach an agreement

      Example Finance told me to raise the price. I asked them to wait one day, and I had my team revise the numbers, so the director let us send it.

      To start: What will you ask finance to do before Friday? ask + sb + to

    13. 13
      A new customer asks for a trial visit.

      A new customer does not know your service yet. He wants a free trial visit before signing anything. Your boss is against giving free services, but you think it can open the door.

      a free trial visit open the door to a contract make a first impression build trust with the customer run an equipment check turn a trial into a contract

      Example My boss told me not to give free services. I suggested that we offer one free inspection, and he let me try it with this customer.

      To start: What do you suggest instead of a free service? suggest + that
