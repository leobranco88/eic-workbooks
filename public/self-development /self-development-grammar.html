What must have really happened?

Every "What did they actually do?" reveal in this module is a guess waiting to happen. This page gives you the language to make that guess properly, in the present and in the past.

The Certainty Scale
MUST
~95% sure
SHOULD
~70% sure
MIGHT / COULD
~50% sure
MAY NOT
~30% sure
CAN'T
~95% sure NOT
Very confidentNot sureVery confident (negative)
01 · Present

Guessing about right now

Use these when you're speculating about a situation happening in the present, something you can't see directly, but can reason about.

modal + base verb / be + -ing / be + adjective

"Must," "might," "could," and "can't" combine directly with the base form. There's no "have" involved here, since you're guessing about now, not about the past.

Must Nina hasn't left the boardroom in two hours. She must be deep into the pitch.
Might / Could Henrique messaged again at 10 PM. He might be testing how fast Rennan replies after hours.
May not Marina hasn't answered the email yet. She may not be checking her inbox right now.
Can't The client's camera has been off the whole call. They can't be fully focused on this meeting.
02 · Past

Guessing about what already happened

This is the version every "What did they actually do?" reveal in the module is quietly asking you to practice.

modal + have + past participle

Same logic, same certainty scale, but now you're reasoning about something that's already over. The evidence is in the past, and so is your guess.

Must have Isabela's proposal had three clear mistakes. She must have rushed the final version.
Should have Draymore replied within a day this time. Rennan's more formal tone should have made the difference.
Might have Théo left the team meeting early. He might have had another call scheduled.
Can't have Rennan built his whole pitch around the co-lead idea. He can't have forgotten to mention it in the debrief.
Watch Out

"Could" and "could have" look like the same word with an extra piece. They're not doing the same job.

COULD = general ability (past)
"Even as a junior, Nina could handle pressure well." (a skill she generally had)
COULD HAVE = a possible past action
"She could have said no, but she negotiated a split instead." (an option that existed, whether or not she took it)
Bonus: Should Have Isn't Always a Guess

In the past-speculation examples above, "should have" was a guess based on evidence. But "should have" has a second job: pointing at a missed opportunity or a regret, no evidence needed, no guessing involved.

Deduction: "Draymore replied fast. Rennan's tone should have made the difference." (I'm reasoning about why something worked.)

Regret: "He should have asked for support earlier." (This was the right move, and it's implied he didn't do it in time.)
Practice

Fill in the gap

Use the certainty scale above to pick the right modal. Some answers are present, some are past, pay attention to the context clue before each sentence.

must be must have should have might have might be can't have can't be could could have
Context: Rennan looked visibly pale right before walking into the director's office.
He ______ nervous about what was coming.
Show answer
must have been — strong evidence (his face), about a past moment.
Context: Henrique's message came in at 10:15 PM, and he's messaged late before too.
He ______ testing how fast Rennan replies outside work hours.
Show answer
might be — a guess about his current intention, not fully certain.
Context: Isabela's client proposal had wrong cost estimates and an unrealistic timeline.
She ______ time to double-check the numbers before sending it.
Show answer
can't have had — the mistakes make it very unlikely she checked carefully.
Context: Looking back at how long Rennan waited before asking for a co-lead.
He ______ asked for support earlier, instead of waiting until the deadline was close.
Show answer
should have — this is regret, not a guess. The right move, taken too late.
Context: Marina hasn't replied to the email in three hours, which isn't like her.
She ______ busy with something urgent right now.
Show answer
must be — strong evidence (unusual silence), about the present.
Context: Nobody knows exactly why Théo left the team meeting fifteen minutes early.
He ______ another call scheduled right after.
Show answer
might have had — a reasonable guess, but no strong evidence either way.
Context: Talking about Nina's general ability under pressure, even early in her career.
Even as a junior, she ______ handle high-pressure situations calmly.
Show answer
could — general past ability, not a guess about a specific event.
Context: Nina ended up negotiating a split with Ricardo instead of a flat yes or no.
She ______ said no outright, but she chose to negotiate instead.
Show answer
could have — a possible action that existed as an option, whether she took it or not.
Context: Rennan built his entire pitch strategy around requesting a co-lead, then never brought it up in the debrief.
That detail ______ slipped his mind. It was central to his whole plan.
Show answer
can't have — very confident this did NOT happen, given how central it was.
Context: Rennan's rewritten email to Draymore, more formal this time, got a reply within a day.
The change in tone ______ made the difference.
Show answer
must have — strong evidence (the fast reply), reasoning about a past cause.
