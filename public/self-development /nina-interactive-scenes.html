Comfort Zone vs. Growth Edge
Zona de conforto vs. limite de crescimento
The pitch room, one hour before the biggest client meeting in the firm's history

Estúdio Braga has been invited to pitch the largest client in the firm's history, a corporate group asking for a full redesign of twelve office locations. Ricardo, the founding partner, wants Nina to lead the pitch alone. He trusts her design instincts completely. She has never led a presentation of this size.

What does Nina do?
What happens

Nina leads solo. The room listens closely, and her design instincts land exactly as Ricardo hoped. Then a client asks a detailed question about structural feasibility across the twelve sites. Nina doesn't have the answer ready, and it shows. She recovers, but Ricardo notices the gap afterward. Confidence carried her far. It didn't cover everything.

reckless calculated risk out of my depth
Talk About It
What makes confidence turn into a blind spot? Is preparation something you can measure, or only notice once it's missing?

What happens

Nina leads the design vision herself, and brings in a senior partner only for the technical feasibility questions. He speaks for about four minutes total. The client signs. Nina gets the credit for the vision, and the safety net for the one part she wasn't ready to own alone. This is what Nina actually did.

stretch assignment co-lead calculated risk
Talk About It
Does asking for a safety net weaken the credit someone gets, or protect it? What's the real difference between support and dependence here?
Try a different choice
What happens

Nina steps back. Ricardo leads, brilliantly, as always. The firm wins the account. Nina returns to her desk afterward, technically fine, nothing went wrong, but a little smaller than she was that morning. Nobody will ever know what she would have done with the room.

Saying No
Dizer não
Ricardo's office, the week after the pitch closed

Ricardo wants Nina leading the Vasconcelos Building project, a heritage restoration of a landmark downtown, with an entirely different set of rules: permits, preservation law, structural constraints she's never worked with. It's the kind of project people remember for decades. Nina is still fully booked from the pitch she just won.

What does Nina do?
What happens

Nina takes on both projects fully. Six weeks in, she's still at her desk at 9 PM two nights in a row, and a factual error slips into a client deck nobody caught in time. Ambition without limits sends a bill. It usually arrives quietly, in the details nobody was watching.

overcommitment bandwidth burnout
Talk About It
At what point does saying yes to everything become a decision to do everything badly? Who usually notices first, the person or the people around them?
Try a different choice
What happens

Nina proposes the split before Ricardo has to ask twice. He agrees, mildly impressed she asked instead of just saying yes to everything. She becomes the person who knows how to protect her own bandwidth without turning down what actually matters. This is what Nina actually did.

assertiveness scope creep negotiating a yes
Talk About It
Is proposing a compromise a form of ambition or a form of caution? Can it honestly be both at the same time?
Try a different choice
What happens

Nina declines. Ricardo doesn't push back. Someone else takes Vasconcelos, and does perfectly fine with it. For a few weeks afterward, Nina finds herself wondering, quietly, whether protecting her time cost her the one project people will still be talking about in ten years.

people-pleasing boundaries
Talk About It
Is protecting your time always the responsible choice, or can it sometimes be a missed opportunity dressed as responsibility?
