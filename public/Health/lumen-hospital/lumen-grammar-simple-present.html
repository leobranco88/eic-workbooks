Simple Present · He, She, It

Affirmative, negative, and questions: the three forms you'll use constantly once you start talking about what other people do, don't do, and whether they do something at all.

1Affirmative Sentences

Talking about what he, she, or it does

He / She / It + verb + s (or -es) affirmative

In the Simple Present, most verbs don't change at all, except when the subject is he, she, or it (or any singular noun that could replace them, like "Dr. Andrade" or "the hospital"). For that subject only, you add -s to the end of the verb.

No Simple Present, a maioria dos verbos não muda em nada, exceto quando o sujeito é he, she ou it (ou qualquer substantivo singular que possa substituí-los, tipo "Dra. Andrade" ou "o hospital"). Só nesse caso, você adiciona -s no final do verbo.

This is easy to forget because with I / you / we / they, the verb stays in its base form. The -s only shows up for third-person singular. That's the whole rule.

É fácil esquecer isso porque com I / you / we / they, o verbo continua na forma base. O -s só aparece na terceira pessoa do singular. Essa é a regra inteira.

A Dra. Andrade examina pacientes todos os dias.
Renata works in the laboratory.
A Renata trabalha no laboratório.

Ellen's tip
A fast way to check subject: if you can replace it with he, she, or it, the verb needs the -s. "The hospital" = it. "Dr. Andrade" = she. "The nurses" = they, so no -s.
Um jeito rápido de checar: se você consegue substituir o sujeito por he, she ou it, o verbo precisa do -s. "The hospital" = it. "Dr. Andrade" = she. "The nurses" = they, sem -s.
2Negative Sentences

Saying what he, she, or it doesn't do

He / She / It + doesn't + base verb negative

Doesn't is short for does not. It carries the "third-person -s" for you, which means the main verb goes back to its plain base form. This is the part almost every student trips over at first: once doesn't is in the sentence, the verb never gets an -s again.

Doesn't é a forma reduzida de does not. Ele já "carrega" o -s da terceira pessoa por você, o que significa que o verbo principal volta pra forma base. É nessa parte que quase todo aluno tropeça no início: uma vez que doesn't está na frase, o verbo nunca mais recebe -s.

Think of it like the -s "moves" from the verb to doesn't. It can only exist in one place in the sentence.

Pense assim: o -s "se muda" do verbo pra doesn't. Ele só pode existir em um lugar da frase por vez.

Common mistake
She doesn't works on Sundays.She doesn't work on Sundays.
The lab doesn't opens at night.The lab doesn't open at night.
Ivone doesn't work on Sundays.
A Ivone não trabalha aos domingos.
Dr. Andrade doesn't rush with her patients.
A Dra. Andrade não apressa os pacientes.
Renata doesn't mind working early shifts.
A Renata não se importa de trabalhar no turno da manhã.
This test doesn't hurt at all.
Esse exame não dói nada.
Ellen's tip
Say the sentence out loud without doesn't first: "She work on Sundays" sounds wrong immediately, right? That's your signal the verb needs to lose the -s once doesn't is added.
Fale a frase em voz alta sem o doesn't primeiro: "She work on Sundays" já soa errado, né? Esse é o sinal de que o verbo precisa perder o -s assim que o doesn't entra.
3Questions

Asking whether he, she, or it does something

Does + he / she / it + base verb ... ? yes/no question

Same logic as negatives: does moves to the front of the sentence and takes the -s with it, so the main verb stays in its base form. This works for both simple yes/no questions and questions that start with a question word (who, what, where, when, why, how).

Mesma lógica das negativas: does vai pro início da frase e leva o -s junto, então o verbo principal fica na forma base. Isso vale tanto pra perguntas simples de sim/não quanto pra perguntas que começam com uma palavra interrogativa (who, what, where, when, why, how).

Does Renata work in the laboratory?
A Renata trabalha no laboratório? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
Does the hospital open on weekends?
O hospital abre nos finais de semana? → Yes, it does. / No, it doesn't.
Does this exam hurt?
Esse exame dói? → No, it doesn't.
What time does Dr. Andrade start work?
Que horas a Dra. Andrade começa a trabalhar?
Where does Renata work?
Onde a Renata trabalha?
Why doesn't Ivone work on Sundays?
Por que a Ivone não trabalha aos domingos?
Common mistake
Does she works on Sundays?Does she work on Sundays?
What time does the lab opens?What time does the lab open?
Ellen's tip
Short answers never repeat the full verb: "Yes, she does" is correct and natural. "Yes, she does work" sounds unnatural in conversation. Native speakers just don't say it that way.
Respostas curtas nunca repetem o verbo completo: "Yes, she does" está certo e soa natural. "Yes, she does work" soa estranho numa conversa. Falantes nativos simplesmente não falam assim.
