The Alarm

By EIC Magazine · 1 min read · Select a passage to mark it. Hover over the dotted words to see them in Portuguese

In September 2026, the OECD published the results of PISA 2025. The headline was bad news for reading: the average score of 15-year-olds fell to a record low for the series.

Between 2015 and 2025, the OECD average in reading fell by 28 points, about one and a half years of learning. Outside the OECD, the fall was 16 points. One in five 15-year-olds is now a low performer in all three areas, up from 16% in 2022. A low performer scores below Level 2, the baseline the OECD uses for taking part in modern life.

Students also struggle with longer, more complex texts, according to a reading of the results by Education Week.

The word “literacy” covers more than one skill. It can mean decoding letters into words, understanding what you read, or using reading in daily life. PISA measures how well students understand and use texts.

The test shows what happened. It does not show why. The OECD itself says that PISA can show a link, but not a cause.

Document B · Brazil and beyond

Three Questions

By EIC Magazine · 1 min read · Select a passage to mark it. Hover over the dotted words to see them in Portuguese

“Literacy” is one word, but it answers three different questions. Mixing them up is the fastest way to lose a debate.

Can children and teenagers read? In Brazil, 49% of 15-year-olds reached Level 2 or above in PISA reading, against 69% in the OECD. The score, 408 points, has been stable since 2015: it did not fall, and it did not rise. The gap inside the country is large. Among the most vulnerable quarter of students, 67% were below the minimum in reading. Among the most advantaged quarter, 26% were. In 2nd grade, the national test Saeb 2023 found 49.3% of children literate, with a margin of 2.8 points. The ministry’s own indicator gives 56%. The two figures come from different measures, so they do not show a rise.

Can adults use reading in daily life? The Brazilian survey Inaf 2024 found that 29% of people aged 15 to 64 are functionally illiterate, the same as in 2018. Among 15-to-29-year-olds, the share went from 14% to 16%.

Do young people read for pleasure? In the UK, the National Literacy Trust found that 36.1% of 8-to-18-year-olds enjoy reading in 2026, up from 32.7% in 2025. The figure is self-reported, and it is still about one in three.

Three questions, three tests, three answers. A fall in one does not prove a fall in another.

67% vs 26%Brazil: most vulnerable and most advantaged quarters below the minimum in reading
36.1%UK young people who enjoy reading in 2026 (32.7% in 2025)

Sources: OECD, PISA 2025 Brazil country note; Todos Pela Educação; Inaf 2024 via UNICEF Brasil and Agência Brasil; Saeb 2023 via O Povo; National Literacy Trust, Children and Young People’s Reading in 2026 and 2025.

Document C · The debate

The Suspects

By EIC Magazine · 1 min read · Select a passage to mark it. Hover over the dotted words to see them in Portuguese

Several suspects are on the table, and none of them has been proved guilty.

The first suspect is the phone. More screen time and less reading for pleasure go together with weaker results. But, as Education Week reports, PISA shows a link, not a cause. Brazil’s Law 15.100 limits the non-educational use of phones in schools. It was signed into law in January 2025, and it does not ban phones: teaching uses are allowed. In 2025, 81% of Brazilian students were in schools with phone restrictions, against 37% in 2022 and 49% across the OECD. The Ministry of Education says this helps explain Brazil’s stable result. That is the ministry’s claim, not a proven effect.

The second suspect is inequality. In Brazil, 67% of the most vulnerable students are below the minimum in reading, against 26% of the most advantaged. Students do not start from the same place.

The third is how reading is taught in the first years of school. The evidence in this dossier is limited: see Louisiana in Document D.

The fourth is the reading habit. In the UK, only about one in three young people say they enjoy reading.

A newer suspect is artificial intelligence, but there is too little evidence to judge it. For now, each suspect has some evidence, and none is proven as the cause.

81% vs 37%Brazilian students in schools with phone restrictions, 2025 and 2022
49%the OECD average for phone restrictions in 2025
Jan 2025when Brazil’s Law 15.100 was signed

Document D · Two cases

Bright Spots, With Caveats

By EIC Magazine · 1 min read · Select a passage to mark it. Hover over the dotted words to see them in Portuguese

Not everything is bad news. Two bright spots stand out, and each one comes with a caveat.

The first is adult literacy. In the OECD adult skills survey, 27 countries were tested in both rounds. Only Finland and Denmark improved significantly. The caveat: the data tell us who improved, not why.

The second is Louisiana. In the US national test for 4th grade reading in 2024, it was the only state above its 2019 level: 216 points, six more than in 2019. The caveat: Louisiana changed teacher training and added reading screeners at the same time, so we cannot isolate one cause.

Neither case shows that the same changes would work in another place. They show where to look.

Document E · Voices

6 people · invented for this lesson

These six people are invented. Their words are not real quotes. Each one stands for a position that is common in the debate.

MA
Marina Alves · 38, 2nd grade teacher in a public school, São Paulo state

I teach children who are seven, and most of them do not have a phone. They need time, books and a teacher who is not alone. By June I know who is going to struggle. The 49.3% for 2nd graders does not surprise me.

PR
Paulo Ribeiro · 51, school coordinator

Since the phone law, students keep their phones in their backpacks, and break time is noisier because they talk more. The ministry says the law helps. It is a claim, and I want evidence before I agree. I cannot tell you that reading went up because of it, and I do not think anyone can yet.

CM
Carla Mendes · 42, mother of a 15-year-old and an 8-year-old

My daughter reads for hours, on her phone. My son will not touch a book. If I take the phone away, will she read more? I doubt it. What I really want to know is whether my son’s school teaches reading well, and how I can compare it with other schools.

LT
Lucas Teixeira · 16, high school student

Everyone says we do not read. I read subtitles, messages and long threads all day. But a whole book? That is hard. Nobody made reading fun for me when I was nine. Phones are not innocent, but I do not think they are the whole story.

HF
Helena Figueiredo · 54, education researcher

Every few years we find a villain. PISA shows a link between screens and weaker results, not a cause. Brazil’s score has been flat since 2015, and 67% against 26% says more about where children start than about any device. I would not build a policy on one number.

RP
Rosana Pires · 46, principal of a municipal school

People talk as if schools did nothing. We teach reading every day, and schools in poor areas start from far behind. Blaming schools is easy. Louisiana changed teacher training and added screeners. Support teachers with training and resources, and then measure.

Phones or foundations?

The motion

Phones are the main reason reading is falling.

One group defends

Phones

Screens take time and attention away from reading.

The other group defends

Foundations

Children must learn to read first, and many do not yet.

How the debate works

  1. 1Sides

    One group defends Phones. The other defends Foundations. The sides are drawn by lot, so nobody defends only what they already think, and the debate is fair.

    2 min

  2. 2Preparation

    Each group chooses 3 facts from the Evidence Bank and writes a claim.

    8 min

  3. 3Round 1

    Each side presents its main argument. No interruptions.

    2 min per side

  4. 4Round 2

    Each side answers the other side’s argument. This is the rebuttal.

    2 min per side

  5. 5Closing

    Each side sums up in 3 sentences.

    1 min per side

  6. 6Verdict

    The observers give their verdict: which side was more convincing, and why?

    3 min

One-to-one class: the teacher takes the side opposite to the student’s. The Evidence Bank is the same.
